## 1. Situation in three sentences

Vivendi SE is the post-December-2024-breakup stub — a Paris-listed holding company owning 9.9% of Universal Music Group, minority stakes in Banijay, Lagardère, MFE and Prisa, plus 100% of Gameloft — trading at ~€2.18 (late-June 2026 reference; exact session unverified) or ~€2.17B market cap against a marked net asset value of roughly €3.7–4.0 per share. The Paris Court of Appeal, sitting in solemn formation after a Cour de cassation remand, will rule on **July 8, 2026** — six days from now — on whether Vincent Bolloré exercised de facto control of Vivendi; a confirmation revives the AMF's July 18, 2025 injunction ordering Bolloré to file a public withdrawal offer (OPR) for all minority shares at a **multi-criteria (i.e., NAV-anchored, not market-price-anchored) fair price**, which the AMF has already stated in writing cannot reference the depressed post-split market price. The court's advocate general opined on ~May 20, 2026 that Bolloré's control "is characterized" (>40% of votes cast at general meetings for years, no resolution ever passed against his will), so the original thesis treated the ruling as a positive-skew binary. The corrected §5 bear inputs imply **~−37–48% downside** from €2.18, against approximately +40% toward a supervised tender path on a win; the original arithmetic did not substantiate the claimed skew.

## 2. Who is selling and why (seller-identity analysis — the core of the edge)

The seller categories below are hypotheses, not transaction-level evidence of motives. Their existence does not rule out rational reassessment of legal risk, governance or asset value:

1. **Burned merger-arbitrage and event funds (the dominant seller).** The stock ran to a 52-week high of **€3.58** around July 18, 2025, when the AMF ordered Bolloré to file the OPR within six months. On **November 28, 2025** the Cour de cassation quashed the Court of Appeal's control finding and remanded; the AMF declared its injunction lapsed on **December 4, 2025**. The arb crowd — positioned for a 2026 tender — liquidated into a no-bid vacuum, driving the stock to the **€1.65** 52-week low. The share register turned over from "holders awaiting a tender" to "nobody's mandate." A stock at €2.18 today, barely above the post-quash low and *despite* a favorable advocate-general opinion in May 2026, tells you the arbs have not come back: they were burned once by exactly this court and will not re-underwrite French litigation risk until the July 8 print. This is a possible event-risk discount; the dossier does not establish that those sellers were uneconomic.
2. **NAV-panic sellers on the UMG drawdown.** Vivendi's UMG stake fell from €4,041M (€22.23/UMG share, Dec 31, 2025) to €3,024M (€16.635, Mar 31, 2026) — a company-disclosed ~€1.0B NAV hit in one quarter. Pershing Square's $63.5–64B takeover proposal (April 7, 2026) briefly repaired the mark (portfolio €5.19B on April 20, UMG at €20.38), but UMG's board **rejected the bid on May 29, 2026** — after Cyrille Bolloré publicly opposed it ("the price is not there at all") — and Pershing then **sold its entire UMG position in early June 2026** (UMG itself bought back €290M at €17.66). Holders who owned Vivendi as a cheap UMG proxy watched their look-through asset whipsaw −25%/+22%/−12% in five months and capitulated.
3. **Structural/mandate sellers.** The stub was ejected from the large-cap universe at the split (pre-split Vivendi was a CAC 40, ~€8.6/share company; the stub started trading at a technical reference of €1.80 — the AMF's own arithmetic: 8.6 − [3.49 Canal+ + 1.80 Havas + 1.20 LHG]). Index funds, large-cap mandates and anyone prohibited from holding a sub-€3B litigation holdco had to leave regardless of price *(index-deletion specifics unverified)*. **Arithmetic correction (September 2026 review):** as written, 8.6 − (3.49 + 1.80 + 1.20) = 8.6 − 6.49 = 2.11, not 1.80, so at least one component reference or the €1.80 stub reference is misstated; none of these figures has been rechecked against the Euronext notice. The €1.80 figure is repeated in §5 (Fair-price mechanics) and does not enter the NAV, scenarios or price triggers.
4. **Who is not selling: the informed side.** Bolloré SE sits immobile at 29.9% (it legally cannot buy without triggering the very offer it is litigating against). Vivendi itself bought back stock (treasury peaked at 8.47% of capital per Bolloré's own AMF filings; 27.9M shares / 2.7% remain after cancellations, 7M shares cancelled March 9, 2026). The activist plaintiff CIAM (0.025% of capital) is litigating, not selling. Note the reflexive detail: **every treasury share Vivendi buys is legally assimilated to Bolloré's stake** under Art. L.233-9 I,2° if control is confirmed — the buyback itself is what pushes Bolloré over 30% and triggers the mandatory offer.

**The edge, stated plainly:** the marginal seller is pricing French appellate procedure (twice bitten), not the assets and not the AMF's already-published position that a tender price must be set by multi-criteria valuation rather than the broken market price. No evidence here establishes what individual sellers believed about realizable NAV, debt or governance costs; a market discount alone does not prove mispricing.

## 3. Business quality & moat

Be honest: **the operating business is mediocre; the "moat" of this investment is legal and positional, not commercial.**

- **Gameloft (100%)** — FY2025 revenue €303M, EBITA €15M (€19M excluding restructuring, +34% YoY); record operating cash flow €31M; Q1 2026 revenue €68M (+1.3% cc), successfully pivoting to PC/console (51% of revenue, +11% cc) with licensed titles (Disney Dreamlight Valley, Nickelodeon, Bluey/BBC). A turnaround, but a sub-scale licensee in a brutal industry. No moat; worth a modest multiple of ~€19M EBITA.
- **UMG (9.91%, 181.8M shares)** — the one genuinely great look-through business: ~1/3 of global recorded music, pricing power via streaming, oligopoly structure. But Vivendi holds it as a passive, pledged, minority stake; the quality accrues to the NAV, not to the stub's operations.
- **Banijay 19.12% / Lagardère 13.38% / MFE 15.92% / Prisa 11.19%** — decent-to-mixed media assets, all minority, all inside the Bolloré orbit.
- **V Collection** (Prisma Media's luxury titles — Harper's Bazaar France, Côté Maison, IDEAT, MilK, The Good Life — acquired March 31, 2026, **from Louis Hachette Group, a Bolloré-controlled related party**, price undisclosed) plus a 13.58% interest in Prisma Group via cash capital increase. Strategically trivial; governance-wise a red flag (see §7).
- The real "moat" of the position: (a) a **court-created claim** — if control is confirmed, French law (Art. L.433-3 CMF, Arts. 234-2 and 236-6 RG AMF) converts minority shares into instruments with a regulator-supervised put; (b) **AMF's published price doctrine for this exact case** (decision 225C1231, fn. 18): the 60-day VWAP rule is inapplicable, the post-split market price is "not pertinent," and the price "must respect multi-criteria valuation elements"; (c) Bolloré's own incentive to eventually own 100% of a vehicle whose main asset is more UMG.

## 5. Valuation: what the price implies, and base / bull / bear intrinsic-value range

**What €2.18 implies.** P/NAV ≈ 0.59 using the corrected €3.72 NAV above. Market capitalization is €2.18 × 995M shares ≈ €2,169M. Assigning all net debt, holdco costs and the contingency to UMG leaves €3,363M − €1,554M − €300M − €30M = €1,479M. The remaining implied value is therefore **€2,169M − €1,479M ≈ €690M** for Banijay + Lagardère + MFE + Prisa + Gameloft/unlisted holdings + the €450M loan, marked at **€4,845M − €3,363M + €289M + €450M = €2,221M**. That is ~31% of those assets' marked value, a substantial discount. This allocation is an analytical illustration, not an observable price for each asset. The original estimate of ~25–35% odds on a near-term NAV-referenced tender was a judgment, not a probability derived from this subtraction. See the §4 arithmetic correction appended to the NAV line: with the corrected €300M holdco drag, NAV is ≈ €3.72/share and the implied value is ≈ €690M (~31%).

- The obligation is twofold: an OPR under **Art. 236-6** (the split = contribution of "the principal assets" — Canal+/Havas/Lagardère/Prisma were 94%+ of revenue and EBITA — decided in principle by the controlling shareholder) **and** a mandatory offer under **Art. 234-2** (control ⇒ Vivendi's treasury shares assimilated to Bolloré ⇒ >30%). The €1.80 reference is subject to the arithmetic correction in §2 item 3.
- Bolloré can pay trivially: Bolloré SE net cash **€5,619M** at Dec 31, 2025 (€8B incl. credit lines); buying 689M minority shares even at €4.00 costs €2.76B. (Press estimates of a "€6–9B" buyout refer to broader perimeters/pre-split framings — **perimeter of that estimate unverified**; the AMF injunction itself covers only Vivendi SE as it exists today.)

## 7. Pre-mortem: the three most likely ways this loses money, and the tripwires/kill criteria

**1. July 8 ruling: no control (est. ~45%).** The new panel, chastened by the Nov 28, 2025 quash, holds that 29.9% + high AGM turnout arithmetic does not equal de facto control. The October §5 component reconciliation implies €1.12–1.37 (approximately −37–48% from €2.18); arbs could stay away for the 2+ years a CIAM cassation appeal would take, and the AMF would have no basis to act under that scenario.
*Historical tripwire:* the adverse ruling invalidates the event thesis. The original instruction to sell immediately and retain a residual position at ≤€1.75 with refinancing is withdrawn; no executed position is documented. **Ratio correction (September 2026 review):** €1.75 / €2.55 bear NAV ≈ **0.69×**, not 0.45×. The historical entry threshold has not been re-underwritten.

**2. Control confirmed but the payoff is delayed-then-diluted: a slow, lowball tender while cash is tunneled.** Sub-risks: (a) Bolloré's cassation appeal pushes closing to late 2027+ — dead money, and UMG could fall meanwhile (at UMG €13, the listed portfolio barely covers debt plus market cap); (b) the independent expert applies a 25–30% holdco discount and lands at ~€2.60–2.90 — precedent risk is real even though the AMF blocked Bolloré's own lowball galaxy OPRs in April 2025; (c) meanwhile the company keeps deploying cash into the family ecosystem: the **€450M loan to Lagardère**, the **undisclosed-price purchase of Prisma Media's luxury titles from Louis Hachette Group**, the **cash capital increase into Prisma Group (13.58%)** — cash spent reduces net cash but may purchase an asset; NAV leakage depends on consideration versus fair value and recoverability, not cash outflow alone. Net debt already crept €1,501M → €1,554M in Q1.
*Tripwires:* any new related-party acquisition or loan >€100M (escalate; test fair value, approval process and recoverability; related-party status alone does not quantify a loss); expert report referencing "holding company discount" >15% (join/support minority challenge — the conformity decision is appealable); UMG <€15 for a quarter (recut NAV); no AMF injunction within 4 months of a favorable ruling.

**3. Balance-sheet accident: the margin-loan structure bites before the legal clock pays.** €1.35B of prepaid forwards/equity swaps mature **September 2026**, secured against UMG/Lagardère/MFE shares with margin calls above 65% LTV. A UMG air-pocket (it printed €16.6 in March) simultaneous with a hostile ruling could force asset sales at the lows or expensive refinancing — the one scenario where the NAV backstop itself erodes.
*Tripwires/kill:* H1 2026 report shows LTV >60% or banks refuse extension past Dec 2027; forced sale of any UMG shares; combined "no control + UMG <€15" requires a full fresh valuation; the original automatic exit/sizing rule is not retained.

(Residual risks, smaller: EC gun-jumping fine materially above ~€30M if computed on a broader turnover base; a squeeze-out never coming because Bolloré is content to sit at 29.9% forever — Odet-galaxy discounts have persisted for decades.)

## 8. Verdict & the price/fact that changes it

**Audit disposition: original PURSUE and all staging/sizing/entry thresholds withdrawn.** The event catalyst failed on July 8. Even before that outcome, the corrected expected-value calculation offered roughly −1% to +4% before costs on an unvalidated 55% success assumption. The historical 2–3% allocation, half-position staging, €2.80–3.00 add, €3.20 chase ceiling and conflicting €1.75/€1.90 re-entry triggers were not supported by a coherent risk/return model.

A new NAV-discount thesis would require current security prices, financing and collateral headroom, realizable after-tax proceeds, related-party exposures and a fresh catalyst assessment. Neither a favourable advocate-general opinion nor regulator-supervised valuation established a probability of winning or a guaranteed NAV payout. No active entry or position size is supplied here.
