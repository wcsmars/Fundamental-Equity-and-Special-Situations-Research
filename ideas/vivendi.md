## 1. Situation in three sentences

Vivendi SE is the post-December-2024-breakup stub — a Paris-listed holding company owning 9.9% of Universal Music Group, minority stakes in Banijay, Lagardère, MFE and Prisa, plus 100% of Gameloft — trading at ~€2.18 (June 27, 2026 close) or ~€2.17B market cap against a marked net asset value of roughly €3.7–4.0 per share. The Paris Court of Appeal, sitting in solemn formation after a Cour de cassation remand, will rule on **July 8, 2026** — six days from now — on whether Vincent Bolloré exercised de facto control of Vivendi; a confirmation revives the AMF's July 18, 2025 injunction ordering Bolloré to file a public withdrawal offer (OPR) for all minority shares at a **multi-criteria (i.e., NAV-anchored, not market-price-anchored) fair price**, which the AMF has already stated in writing cannot reference the depressed post-split market price. The court's advocate general opined on ~May 20, 2026 that Bolloré's control "is characterized" (>40% of votes cast at general meetings for years, no resolution ever passed against his will), so the original thesis treated the ruling as a positive-skew binary. The corrected §5 bear inputs imply **~−36–47% downside** from €2.18, against approximately +40% toward a supervised tender path on a win; the original arithmetic did not substantiate the claimed skew.

## 2. Who is selling and why (seller-identity analysis — the core of the edge)

The sellers here are unusually identifiable, and almost none of them are selling on a view of asset value:

1. **Burned merger-arbitrage and event funds (the dominant seller).** The stock ran to a 52-week high of **€3.58** around July 18, 2025, when the AMF ordered Bolloré to file the OPR within six months. On **November 28, 2025** the Cour de cassation quashed the Court of Appeal's control finding and remanded; the AMF declared its injunction lapsed on **December 4, 2025**. The arb crowd — positioned for a 2026 tender — liquidated into a no-bid vacuum, driving the stock to the **€1.65** 52-week low. The share register turned over from "holders awaiting a tender" to "nobody's mandate." A stock at €2.18 today, barely above the post-quash low and *despite* a favorable advocate-general opinion in May 2026, tells you the arbs have not come back: they were burned once by exactly this court and will not re-underwrite French litigation risk until the July 8 print. That is a classic uneconomic-seller gap.
2. **NAV-panic sellers on the UMG drawdown.** Vivendi's UMG stake fell from €4,041M (€22.23/UMG share, Dec 31, 2025) to €3,024M (€16.635, Mar 31, 2026) — a company-disclosed ~€1.0B NAV hit in one quarter. Pershing Square's $63.5–64B takeover proposal (April 7, 2026) briefly repaired the mark (portfolio €5.19B on April 20, UMG at €20.38), but UMG's board **rejected the bid on May 29, 2026** — after Cyrille Bolloré publicly opposed it ("the price is not there at all") — and Pershing then **sold its entire UMG position in early June 2026** (UMG itself bought back €290M at €17.66). Holders who owned Vivendi as a cheap UMG proxy watched their look-through asset whipsaw −25%/+22%/−12% in five months and capitulated.
3. **Structural/mandate sellers.** The stub was ejected from the large-cap universe at the split (pre-split Vivendi was a CAC 40, ~€8.6/share company; the stub started trading at a technical reference of €1.80 — the AMF's own arithmetic: 8.6 − [3.49 Canal+ + 1.80 Havas + 1.20 LHG]). Index funds, large-cap mandates and anyone prohibited from holding a sub-€3B litigation holdco had to leave regardless of price *(index-deletion specifics unverified)*.
4. **Who is not selling: the informed side.** Bolloré SE sits immobile at 29.9% (it legally cannot buy without triggering the very offer it is litigating against). Vivendi itself bought back stock (treasury peaked at 8.47% of capital per Bolloré's own AMF filings; 27.9M shares / 2.7% remain after cancellations, 7M shares cancelled March 9, 2026). The activist plaintiff CIAM (0.025% of capital) is litigating, not selling. Note the reflexive detail: **every treasury share Vivendi buys is legally assimilated to Bolloré's stake** under Art. L.233-9 I,2° if control is confirmed — the buyback itself is what pushes Bolloré over 30% and triggers the mandatory offer.

**The edge, stated plainly:** the marginal seller is pricing French appellate procedure (twice bitten), not the assets and not the AMF's already-published position that a tender price must be set by multi-criteria valuation rather than the broken market price. Nobody who sold at €1.65–2.20 did so because they thought 182M UMG shares, €450M of Lagardère receivables and €1.5B of other listed stakes were worth less than €2.2B plus €1.55B of debt.

## 3. Business quality & moat

Be honest: **the operating business is mediocre; the "moat" of this investment is legal and positional, not commercial.**

- **Gameloft (100%)** — FY2025 revenue €303M, EBITA €15M (€19M excluding restructuring, +34% YoY); record operating cash flow €31M; Q1 2026 revenue €68M (+1.3% cc), successfully pivoting to PC/console (51% of revenue, +11% cc) with licensed titles (Disney Dreamlight Valley, Nickelodeon, Bluey/BBC). A turnaround, but a sub-scale licensee in a brutal industry. No moat; worth a modest multiple of ~€19M EBITA.
- **UMG (9.91%, 181.8M shares)** — the one genuinely great look-through business: ~1/3 of global recorded music, pricing power via streaming, oligopoly structure. But Vivendi holds it as a passive, pledged, minority stake; the quality accrues to the NAV, not to the stub's operations.
- **Banijay 19.12% / Lagardère 13.38% / MFE 15.92% / Prisa 11.19%** — decent-to-mixed media assets, all minority, all inside the Bolloré orbit.
- **V Collection** (Prisma Media's luxury titles — Harper's Bazaar France, Côté Maison, IDEAT, MilK, The Good Life — acquired March 31, 2026, **from Louis Hachette Group, a Bolloré-controlled related party**, price undisclosed) plus a 13.58% interest in Prisma Group via cash capital increase. Strategically trivial; governance-wise a red flag (see §7).
- The real "moat" of the position: (a) a **court-created claim** — if control is confirmed, French law (Art. L.433-3 CMF, Arts. 234-2 and 236-6 RG AMF) converts minority shares into instruments with a regulator-supervised put; (b) **AMF's published price doctrine for this exact case** (decision 225C1231, fn. 18): the 60-day VWAP rule is inapplicable, the post-split market price is "not pertinent," and the price "must respect multi-criteria valuation elements"; (c) Bolloré's own incentive to eventually own 100% of a vehicle whose main asset is more UMG.

## 5. Valuation: what the price implies, and base / bull / bear intrinsic-value range

**What €2.18 implies.** P/NAV ≈ 0.58 using the €3.74 NAV above. Market capitalization is €2.18 × 995M shares ≈ €2,169M. Assigning all net debt, holdco costs and the contingency to UMG leaves €3,363M − €1,554M − €275M − €30M = €1,504M. The remaining implied value is therefore **€2,169M − €1,504M ≈ €665M** for Banijay + Lagardère + MFE + Prisa + Gameloft/unlisted holdings + the €450M loan, marked at **€4,845M − €3,363M + €289M + €450M = €2,221M**. That is ~30% of those assets' marked value, a substantial discount. This allocation is an analytical illustration, not an observable price for each asset. The original estimate of ~25–35% odds on a near-term NAV-referenced tender was a judgment, not a probability derived from this subtraction.

**Intrinsic-value range (per share, 995M shares):**

**Arithmetic correction:** €2.55 NAV × (1 − 45–55% discount) gives €1.1475–1.4025, approximately €1.15–1.40. The original €1.40–1.70 bear range and −20–25% downside claim were inconsistent with those inputs. Using the corrected bear range in the original probability calculation below reduces expected value to approximately the entry price; this does not validate the probability assumptions or re-underwrite the thesis.

**Fair-price mechanics (the legal core, from [AMF decision 225C1231 of July 18, 2025](https://bdif.amf-france.org/back/api/v1/documents/2025/225C1231/92DC7EC5C5114CB6B99E8DA044ED3F1120E969BC6EB21454556D78A768E97B21.pdf), especially p. 10, footnote 18):**
- The obligation is twofold: an OPR under **Art. 236-6** (the split = contribution of "the principal assets" — Canal+/Havas/Lagardère/Prisma were 94%+ of revenue and EBITA — decided in principle by the controlling shareholder) **and** a mandatory offer under **Art. 234-2** (control ⇒ Vivendi's treasury shares assimilated to Bolloré ⇒ >30%). All three of Bolloré's derogation requests were refused.
- **Price:** the 60-day VWAP rule does not apply (Bolloré lacks majority). AMF fn.18 verbatim logic: the post-split market price is *not a pertinent reference* because the company was "amputated of practically two-thirds of its value" (Euronext technical reference €1.80 on Dec 16, 2024 vs €8.6 pre-split); the price must satisfy **multi-criteria valuation** under Art. 234-6 — for a holdco of listed stakes, that is NAV-anchored, with an independent expert (Art. 261-1) and AMF conformity review, both appealable by CIAM et al. to the Cour d'appel.
- Bolloré can pay trivially: Bolloré SE net cash **€5,619M** at Dec 31, 2025 (€8B incl. credit lines); buying 689M minority shares even at €4.00 costs €2.76B. (Press estimates of a "€6–9B" buyout refer to broader perimeters/pre-split framings — **perimeter of that estimate unverified**; the AMF injunction itself covers only Vivendi SE as it exists today.)

**Expected value at €2.18 into July 8 (my estimates):** P(control confirmed) ≈ 55% (advocate general supportive on vote-arithmetic grounds — Bolloré 42.95% of votes cast at the Dec 2024 EGM, >40% for years, never defeated — which is the orthodox L.233-3 I,4°-style de facto test and survives the Cass's rejection of "reputation/personal authority" reasoning; but the Cass has already quashed once and the new panel may be gun-shy). With the corrected bear range, EV ≈ 0.55×€3.00 + 0.45×€1.1475–1.4025 ≈ **€2.17–2.28 (roughly −1% to +5% versus €2.18, before costs)**. The midpoint is approximately €2.22 (+2%). The winning branch still assumes a path to €3.3–4.5 by 2027–28, but this calculation does not substantiate the original +11% immediate expected return.

## 7. Pre-mortem: the three most likely ways this loses money, and the tripwires/kill criteria

**1. July 8 ruling: no control (est. ~45%).** The new panel, chastened by the Nov 28, 2025 quash, holds that 29.9% + high AGM turnout arithmetic does not equal de facto control. The §5 bear inputs imply €1.15–1.40 (approximately −36–47% from €2.18); arbs could stay away for the 2+ years a CIAM cassation appeal would take, and the AMF would have no basis to act under that scenario.
*Tripwire/kill:* the ruling itself. If "no control," **sell the event tranche immediately**; the original residual-position threshold was ≤€1.75 *and* structured financing refinanced past 2027. **Ratio correction:** €1.75 / €2.55 bear NAV ≈ **0.69×**, not 0.45×. The historical entry threshold has not been re-underwritten.

**2. Control confirmed but the payoff is delayed-then-diluted: a slow, lowball tender while cash is tunneled.** Sub-risks: (a) Bolloré's cassation appeal pushes closing to late 2027+ — dead money, and UMG could fall meanwhile (at UMG €13, the listed portfolio barely covers debt plus market cap); (b) the independent expert applies a 25–30% holdco discount and lands at ~€2.60–2.90 — precedent risk is real even though the AMF blocked Bolloré's own lowball galaxy OPRs in April 2025; (c) meanwhile the company keeps deploying cash into the family ecosystem: the **€450M loan to Lagardère**, the **undisclosed-price purchase of Prisma Media's luxury titles from Louis Hachette Group**, the **cash capital increase into Prisma Group (13.58%)** — every euro that leaves as related-party consideration is a euro the expert can't count as net cash. Net debt already crept €1,501M → €1,554M in Q1.
*Tripwires:* any new related-party acquisition or loan >€100M (escalate; this is NAV leakage with the meter running); expert report referencing "holding company discount" >15% (join/support minority challenge — the conformity decision is appealable); UMG <€15 for a quarter (recut NAV); no AMF injunction within 4 months of a favorable ruling.

**3. Balance-sheet accident: the margin-loan structure bites before the legal clock pays.** €1.35B of prepaid forwards/equity swaps mature **September 2026**, secured against UMG/Lagardère/MFE shares with margin calls above 65% LTV. A UMG air-pocket (it printed €16.6 in March) simultaneous with a hostile ruling could force asset sales at the lows or expensive refinancing — the one scenario where the NAV backstop itself erodes.
*Tripwires/kill:* H1 2026 report shows LTV >60% or banks refuse extension past Dec 2027; forced sale of any UMG shares; combined "no control + UMG <€15" = **exit entirely**, thesis dead on both legs.

(Residual risks, smaller: EC gun-jumping fine materially above ~€30M if computed on a broader turnover base; a squeeze-out never coming because Bolloré is content to sit at 29.9% forever — Odet-galaxy discounts have persisted for decades.)

## 8. Verdict & the price/fact that changes it

**Historical verdict: PURSUE — as a special situation, not a compounder: illustrative maximum 2–3% of portfolio value, staged. The corrected §5 arithmetic weakens this original verdict; the sizing and entry thresholds below have not been re-underwritten.** At €2.18 you pay ~0.58× a conservatively marked NAV, the identifiable sellers are non-economic (twice-burned arbs and UMG-drawdown capitulators), the regulator has already committed in writing to a multi-criteria fair price that cannot use the broken market print, the obligated buyer has €5.6B of net cash, and the advocate general has endorsed the control finding on grounds compatible with the supreme court's own doctrine. The honest caveats: this is a coin-flip court date in six days, the operating stub is poor quality, the controller has both the incentive and the demonstrated toolkit (related-party deals, galaxy-style lowballs) to make minorities wait and take less, and even the winning branch likely pays out in late 2027.

**Staging:** half position now (the event premium is not in the price — €2.18 sits 39% below the July 2025 AMF-injunction high); add on a control-confirmation ruling even at €2.80–3.00 (still ~20–25% below a defensible €3.60+ tender corridor, with the remaining risk being price-setting rather than obligation-existence).

**The fact that changes it:** the July 8, 2026 ruling. "No control" → exit the event tranche same day; re-underwrite only as a pure deep-discount NAV position at ≤€1.75 with financing derisked. **The price that changes it:** above ~€3.20 pre-ruling the skew inverts (you'd be paying for the win) — don't chase; below ~€1.90 post-adverse-ruling with financing extended, the pure-value case reopens on its own merits.

## 9. Verification checklist (exact documents to read next, and what to look for)

1. **Paris Court of Appeal ruling, July 8, 2026** (when published; case remanded from Cass. com. pourvoi n° 25-14.362, Nov 28, 2025 — full text on courdecassation.fr). Look for: whether control is found under L.233-3; the *date* control is deemed established (drives when the offer obligation crystallized); whether the panel adopts the AG's vote-arithmetic reasoning (cassation-proof) or something more assailable.
2. **AMF décision 225C1231 (July 18, 2025)** — already obtained and read; re-read fn.18 with counsel: multi-criteria price requirement, inapplicability of 60-day VWAP, Art. 234-6 adjustment powers. Template for the re-issued injunction.
3. **Vivendi Universal Registration Document 2025** (filed with AMF; on vivendi.com — site had TLS issues, use AMF BDIF/info-financiere.fr). Look for: note on borrowings (exact structured-financing terms, LTV, maturity/extension status, which shares pledged and how many); Lagardère €450M loan terms and security; UMG equity-method carrying value vs the 181,799k-share market value; treasury-share allocation (cancellation vs performance plans); related-party transactions note (V Collection price paid to Louis Hachette Group, Prisma Group capital increase amount, any Bolloré current-account balances); litigation provisions for the EC gun-jumping probe; exact Bolloré SE share count and voting rights.
4. **H1 2026 financial report (~end July 2026).** Look for: net debt roll (cash cost of V Collection + dividend), refinancing of the Sept 2026 maturity, updated portfolio marks, any post-July-8 subsequent-events note (including a re-issued AMF injunction).
5. **AMF BDIF filings on FR0000127771 after July 8** — the new injunction (if any): filing deadline, whether closing is again conditioned on the cassation outcome, and any derogation re-requests by Bolloré.
6. **Independent expert report + offer document (note d'information), when the OPR is filed.** Look for: NAV methodology, treatment of holdco discount and tax on embedded gains, UMG reference price window, treatment of the Lagardère loan and unlisted assets — this is where the fight over €3.00 vs €4.50 happens; check CIAM/minority responses and any conformity appeal.

8. **UMG H1 2026 results (late July, unverified date)** — the NAV driver: streaming/subscription trajectory post-Ackman-rejection; any strategic-interest disclosures; the board's stated view that $64B "fundamentally and materially undervalues" UMG (May 29, 2026 release) as a bull-case anchor for the 182M shares.
9. **Cross-check TIM proceeds** (Poste Italiane releases: €684M for 15% + €187M for residual 2.51%) against Vivendi's FY25 cash-flow statement to pin down the middle-tranche market sales and reconcile the ~€1.07B 2025 debt reduction.
