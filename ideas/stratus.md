## 1. Situation in three sentences

Stratus Properties, a 30+ year Austin residential/mixed-use developer, adopted a stockholder-approved (June 1, 2026; 4,905,081 for vs 5,612 against) plan of complete liquidation, with management estimating total distributions of $29.73–$37.69/share (Eastdil Secured reasonableness opinion, March 24, 2026) against a fully-diluted count of 8,179,774 shares — $243M–$308M total — with the high end assuming asset sales complete within 24 months of approval and the low end within 36 months. A $5.00/share initial distribution (record July 13, pay July 20) leaves an estimated $24.73–$32.69/share of remaining distributions against a ~$21.05 stub, but the company will voluntarily delist from Nasdaq (trading suspension pre-open ~August 10, 2026, Form 25 ~July 31) and deregister with the SEC (Form 15 immediately suspends 10-K/10-Q/8-K obligations), with **no OTC listing arranged and no commitment to post-deregistration disclosure**. The bet is therefore simple to state: you are paying ~85% of the *low end* of management's remaining-distribution estimate to hold an unlisted, non-reporting Austin real-estate stub for 1–3 years, with a hypothesis of mandate-driven selling that does not establish seller motives or rule out informed selling.

## 2. Who is selling and why (seller-identity analysis — the core of the edge)

Forced selling is a hypothesis to test against index notices, holdings changes and fund mandates; the dossier does not establish the motives of individual sellers.

**(a) Index funds — mechanically forced.** STRS has been dropped from the Russell 2000 and Russell 2000 Defensive indexes (reported by MarketScreener in early July 2026, coincident with the delisting announcement; exact deletion date unverified). Index trackers cannot hold an unlisted, deregistering security; they must be out before or at the August 10 suspension. Vanguard and other passive holders filed 13G/A amendments in March–April 2026 showing reduced stakes. A confirmed index deletion can require selling, but execution timing and price sensitivity vary. The precise deletion notice, affected holdings and mandates have not been verified. **Correction (September 2026 review):** August 8, 2026 is a Saturday; with trading suspended before the open on Monday, August 10, the last session is Friday, August 7, the ~Aug 7 cutoff used in §6 and §8.

**(b) Oasis Management (the activist that got the outcome it wanted) — unwinding, but read it carefully.** Oasis (Seth Fischer, HK), 13D holder since September 2016, held 1,158,426 sh (14.5%) in Feb 2024 and 1,116,095 sh (14.0%) as of April 13, 2026. Per its July 7 13D/A (Amendment 7) and July 9 Form 4: sold 720 @ $30.03 (5/7), 10,000 @ $29.05 (5/26), 10,000 @ $28.90 (6/30), a 117,612-share block @ $28.03 on July 2 — the day after the delisting announcement — then 58,720 @ ~$27.50 (7/7–7/8), taking it to 784,797 sh (9.8%). Press tallies put Oasis's trailing-six-month selling at ~290K shares (~$8.45M) across 23 trades. Interpretation: Oasis campaigned for years for exactly this liquidation; selling at $28.03 cum (≈$23.03 stub) is consistent with a hedge fund that cannot mark or redeem against a dark 3-year stub and is harvesting the win — a possible *mandate/liquidity* seller; no cited mandate establishes this motive. The caveat: Oasis has better information than any outside buyer (it saw the process from inside via years of engagement), and it is accepting ~93% of the low-end remaining value rather than waiting. The filings alone cannot distinguish liquidity needs from concern about timing or proceeds. Their remaining ~785K shares are a continuing overhang through August 8. **Correction (September 2026 review):** the overhang runs through the last session, Friday, August 7; August 8 is a Saturday (see §2(a)).

**(c) Mandate-constrained institutions.** Ingalls & Snyder (13.7%, advisory accounts) and other RIAs face account-level restrictions on unlisted/non-reporting securities; retail brokers increasingly refuse to transact in Expert Market names, which pressures anyone who might ever need to sell to do it now.

**(d) Insiders — small.** Director James E. Joseph filed Form 144s to sell 12,335 sh (~$361K, May 18) and 3,380 sh (~$94K, June 3). Form 144 is a notice of proposed sales, not proof of execution; neither post-vesting purpose nor informational content is established here. No 144s from Armstrong or Pickens found.

**Why non-informational selling was hypothesized:** the seller with the deadline (indexes) doesn't do valuation at all; the activist seller is exiting *after* winning its campaign, into a structural liquidity event it helped create; and the price decline ($28.76 on July 2 → $26.05 on July 10, i.e., stub $23.76 → $21.05) coincided with the distribution, delisting and Oasis filings. Loss of liquidity and reporting is economically material, and this review cannot exclude other information or valuation-driven selling. The natural buyer base (dark-stub/liquidation specialists) is small and disciplined, which is why the discount can persist until the payments themselves close the gap. **Correction (September 2026 review):** the backlog dates the $28.76 reference price 7/1/26, not July 2 (the date the backlog was started); the one-day difference does not change the analysis.

## 3. Business quality & moat

Not a going concern — quality here means *asset quality, realizability, and alignment*:

- **Asset quality: genuinely above-average for a liquidation.** ~1,500 acres concentrated in Barton Creek / Southwest Austin (Holden Hills Phases 1–2, Circle C, Lantana), two new luxury multifamily assets (The Saint George, 316 units, Burnet Rd, completed 2025, in lease-up; The Saint June, 182 units, Barton Creek, stabilized), The Annie B downtown land, and scattered retail/land remnants. No office exposure ("no commercial office space" per company). Entitled land in the Barton Creek corridor is scarce and hard to replicate — this was always the moat, and it survives into the liquidation as pricing power on the land sales.
- **Realizability is bimodal.** Stabilized income assets (retail already sold; the two multifamily assets have Feb–Mar 2026 broker opinions of value and an improving Austin multifamily market) are sellable inside 12–18 months. The land — especially Holden Hills — is the tail: Eastdil valued Holden Hills on a **12-year DCF** of lot sales (explicitly "solely as a valuation methodology"), which tells you a single bulk buyer at full value is not assumed to exist. Lot sale contracts for Phase 1's ~475 home sites were expected to begin late March 2026 (status since: unverified).
- **Structural complexity taxes the value.** Most remaining assets sit in JVs: Holden Hills Ph1 50%, Holden Hills Ph2 50%, The Saint June 34.13% (up to 54.13% via promote), The Annie B 31%, and critically **The Saint George only 10%** (rising to 20/30/50% through hurdles) — the consolidated $52.4M Saint George loan and most of its equity belong to partners. NCI on the balance sheet is $138.7M vs $211.7M of Stratus equity. Management's range already nets JV waterfalls, but every sale needs partner and lender consents (Fifth Third consent is required for any distribution — obtained for the $5.00, needed again for each future one).
- **Execution track record is decent:** 2025–2026 sales (West Killeen, Lantana Place $57.5M, Kingwood Place $60.8M, Jones Crossing retail $46.5M) closed at or above plan — Jones Crossing produced ~$21.7M net vs ~$20.0M estimated at contract signing. Austin backdrop (mid-2026): multifamily vacancy down 90bps to ~11.5% with 2026 deliveries halving to ~10,200 units and rent growth returning; office still 26–27% vacant but irrelevant to STRS.

## 5. Valuation: what the price implies, and base / bull / bear intrinsic-value range

**Stub price ~$21.05 vs remaining distributions $24.73 (low) / $28.71 (mid) / $32.69 (high).** The market price equals **85% of the low end** — you are paying nothing for the mid or high case, and the low end itself already assumes the *slow* (36-month) timeline.

**Reproducible timing illustrations (October 1 review):** the original ~8–54% IRRs did not specify payment dates and are withdrawn. With a $21.05 purchase at month 0, the annual effective IRR below solves `21.05 = Σ payment_m / (1 + r)^(m/12)`. Equal monthly distributions start at month 1; terminal distributions occur only at the final month. No fees, taxes, losses or reinvestment returns are included.

A 15% miss below the remaining low estimate gives $21.0205, a 0.999× MOIC and −0.04% annualized if all paid at month 42. This is an assumed payout, **not a bear floor**; §4 can produce lower proceeds before tax.

## 7. Pre-mortem: the three most likely ways this loses money, and the tripwires/kill criteria

**(1) Holden Hills clears low or slow (the single biggest swing).** Eastdil's 12-year DCF framing concedes there is no natural bulk buyer at full value; the ETJ Law (SB 2038) that underpins the density upside is still under constitutional challenge by 20+ Texas cities with no final ruling; Austin land/lot demand could roll over with rates. If Ph1 sells lot-by-lot into 2029+ and Ph2's $95.7M agreed value proves to be a 2025-vintage mark, total distributions land below $29.73 and the tail sits in a liquidating trust. *Tripwires:* no announced Holden Hills Ph1 bulk/lot-sale volume by mid-2027; ETJ law struck down; Ph2 partner disputes. *Monitoring trigger (not an assured exit):* cumulative distributions < $10/share (beyond the initial $5.00) by December 2027.

**(2) The dark-stub trap — you can't get out and can't see in.** After ~Aug 10 there is no exchange market, no arranged OTC quotation, and no SEC reporting; the company made **no commitment to shareholder reporting post-deregistration** (checked the July 1 press release and 8-K — silent). If the thesis sours, an exit may be unavailable; no comparable evidence supports the original 20–40% exit-haircut estimate. Mitigation is entirely ex-ante: size it so zero liquidity for 36 months is acceptable, and demand the discount now. *Tripwire (pre-purchase):* call IR this week and ask whether they will post periodic net-asset/distribution updates to holders (transfer-agent mailings or website); a "no" materially weakens the case. *Kill criterion:* none post-purchase — this risk cannot be managed after Aug 10, only priced.

**(3) Cost, tax and waterfall leakage compresses the low end.** C-corp federal tax + Texas margin tax on appreciated sales; G&A and retention running longer than modeled (management's own risk factors flag exactly this); the $9M+ NEO severance; JV frictions — Saint George is 90% third-party (Stratus's 10% + promote could round to little if hurdles miss), Annie B operating loans ($8.9M) are recoverable only ahead of *distributions*, not ahead of the $11.8M land loan, so a weak downtown land price impairs them; lender consents (Fifth Third) required for every distribution; Delaware contingency reserves held for unknown liabilities. *Tripwires:* any disclosed reserve > ~$25M at trust formation; Annie B sold below ~$21M gross; Saint George sold with Stratus receiving < $3M. *Kill criterion:* evidence (from distribution sizes) that realized net proceeds are tracking >15% below the low-end pace by mid-2027.

Also worth naming: **abandonment/dividend-recharacterization risk** (Board can abandon the plan; prior distributions would become taxable dividends) — remote post-dissolution-filing but nonzero; and **ex-date risk** — the displayed $5 price adjustment creates no economic bargain by itself; the original $21.50 cap has been withdrawn.
