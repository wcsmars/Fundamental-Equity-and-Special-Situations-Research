## 1. Situation in three sentences

Three years of rate-shock deratings, UK wealth-manager consolidation, and activist pressure (Saba, AVI) have forced the boards of a cluster of London-listed trusts to legislate their own discounts away — via continuation votes, tenders at 2.5–10% discounts to NAV, realisation share classes, and outright wind-ups — on published timetables running from July 2026 through 2027+. The trade is buying discounts of 5–53% and being paid out at 90–98% of NAV by mechanical corporate actions rather than by market re-rating. The catch, confirmed by this work: two of the six legs no longer have live catalysts, the deepest discount (SEIT) sits behind 75% gearing, and the two private-equity names carry NAVs that are 86%+ two-quarters stale, so the harvestable spread after fees, pro-ration, time, and (for a US buyer) PFIC/stamp friction is far smaller than the headline discounts suggest.

## 2. Who is selling and why (seller-identity analysis — the core of the edge)

The quality of this trade differs name by name precisely because the seller identity differs:

- **HVPE — structural, non-economic sellers; the strongest setup.** The selling since 2022 is UK wealth-manager consolidation (merged firms dumping sub-scale trust lines), the UK cost-disclosure regime that made fund-of-funds look grotesquely expensive on paper (HVPE's 2.17% ongoing includes underlying HarbourVest fund fees), and multi-asset mandates exiting "illiquids" wholesale. These sellers do not have a view on HarbourVest's portfolio. The buyers are informed: Saba doubled to **10.06% of voting rights on July 6, 2026** (mostly via total return swap), a week before the vote; AVI holds 3.1%. Caveat: this also means the trade is *crowded* — you are buying alongside, not ahead of, the activists.
- **PEY — sellers may be partly right.** Same structural LPE fatigue, aggravated by an orphaned EUR quote line on the LSE, sub-scale size (€0.8bn net assets at April 30, 2026), and genuinely poor performance (QuotedData: an ~11% five-year loss, worst tier of the AIC Private Equity sector). When the seller's complaint is manager skill and the "fix" leaves 70%+ of capital with the same manager at 150bps, seller identity gives less edge.
- **EOT — time-and-process arbitrage only.** The base has already rotated to arbitrageurs after the wind-up announcement (shares jumped to a 6.5% discount in May; 47.8% of holders pre-committed support). Today's remaining ~2–3 point spread is compensation for six weeks of completion/beta risk, sold to you by (a) platforms and retail brokers that force-sell rather than process s110 scheme elections, and (b) mandates that cannot hold a delisting/liquidating vehicle. Real but small edge.
- **SEIT — the cleanest forced seller in the cluster.** The June 2026 dividend suspension ("no regular dividends until the RCF is significantly repaid") mechanically evicted income funds and yield-screen retail — holders whose mandate, not their analysis, made them sell (shares fell 19% on the announcement). A 53% discount created by eviction, with Saba at 21.6% policing the wind-down, is the right *shape* of opportunity; the question is asset value and leverage, not seller identity (§4).
- **GDV — the informed money is now the seller.** Saba lost the June 29 vote, has cut its stake to ~5.35% (June 11 filing showed a further 390,926-share reduction), and Gabelli's majority-of-outstanding voting standard plus insider/preferred voting power make GDV effectively activist-proof. When the activist who anchored your thesis is selling, the seller-identity edge has inverted. Kill.
- **VEIL — mixed and partly informed.** Frontier-mandate funds must exit before Vietnam leaves the FTSE Frontier index (September 2026), a genuine non-economic seller. But **70.06% of shares outstanding were tendered** into the July 10% tender — the holder base overwhelmingly wants out at 2.5% below NAV after a +46.6% twelve-month VN-Index run to record highs (~1,929). That is closer to informed distribution than forced selling.

## 3. Business quality & moat

These are funds; "quality" = asset quality + NAV credibility + governance.

- **HVPE:** ~$4.4bn (£ equivalent) diversified global buyout/growth fund-of-funds via HarbourVest — a top-decile PE platform with real access moat. Asset quality good; structure (double fee layer) is the permanent defect the discount prices. Governance now shareholder-aligned under duress: no new commitments in 2026, distribution pool gets 100% of realizations ($70.7M co-investment sale July 1; $300M secondary sale December 2025), 5–10% of NAV committed annually to distributions until a second continuation vote by July 2029.
- **PEY:** Partners Group direct/co-investment book, EUR-denominated. Manager is blue-chip but this vehicle's five-year record is poor; incentive fees survive the restructuring on both classes (125bps on realisation shares vs 150bps continuing). Mediocre.
- **EOT:** Liquid, large/mid-cap listed European equities (Darwall's concentrated book). NAV is daily, real, and verifiable — the only NAV in the cluster with zero credibility discount. The "business" ends in August.
- **SEIT:** Esoteric energy-efficiency infrastructure (CHP, on-site generation; US steel-linked Primary Energy, etc.). Portfolio generated £84M cash in FY26 (vs £97M prior) and management insists assets "perform in line with expectations," but the equity sits behind consolidated gearing of **83.1% of NAV at March 31, 2026 (74.5% pro-forma post-disposal)** vs a 65% policy limit. Quality of assets: middling and hard to price; quality of balance sheet: poor — that is what 53% is paying you for.
- **GDV:** Perfectly fine diversified US large-cap equity CEF with cheap structural leverage (preferreds) and a 6.1% managed distribution. No moat, no event.
- **VEIL:** $1.4bn+ (£1.05bn mcap) Dragon Capital flagship; best-in-class local research access to Vietnamese banks/property/retail. Underlying is listed, NAV daily and credible. Quality is real; the risk is the asset class's temperature, not the vehicle.

## 4. Normalized owner earnings & balance sheet (arithmetic shown; unverified inputs flagged)

For discount vehicles the "owner earnings" question becomes: **what is the real NAV, what does it cost to wait, and how much of the spread is actually harvestable?**

**HVPE** — Price 3,315p; stated NAV 4,450p (May 31 estimate).
- NAV staleness (from the March 31, 2026 estimate methodology): **6% actual Mar-26 marks (publics), 86% Dec-25 marks, 8% Sep-25 marks.** The May estimate rolls these forward only for FX/flows. Neither the Dec-25 $300M secondary sale nor the July $70.7M co-investment sale had pricing-vs-carrying-value disclosed (verification item #1). Secondary-market pricing for buyout FoF stakes has generally run ~85–95% of NAV (unverified for 2026); haircutting stated NAV 10% gives a "real NAV" of ~4,005p → the *real* discount is ~17%, not 25.5%.
- Cost of carry: 2.17% ongoing (incl. underlying fund fees) — call it ~90p/year on today's NAV, ~2.9% of the purchase price. The discount must close ~2.2 points/year just to pay the fee drag.
- Harvestable events: $400M tender at ~10% discount to NAV ≈ 4,005p equivalent, i.e. **+20.8% vs today's price on whatever is accepted**. $400M ÷ (~$3.1bn current market cap × 1.208 tender/current-price ratio) ≈ **10.7% of outstanding shares** (£2,310M × 1.35, FX unverified). If 85% of outstanding shares are tendered, acceptance ≈ 10.7% / 85% ≈ **12.6%** → accepted-slice premium ≈ 12.6% × 20.8% ≈ **+2.6% of the initial position value**. Separately, retiring 2.5% of outstanding shares at a 25% NAV discount adds **~0.64% to NAV/share**: (1 − 0.025 × 0.75) / (1 − 0.025) − 1. That share-retirement assumption has not been reconciled to the ~$100M remaining return budget.
- Balance sheet: modest; HVPE historically runs a credit facility against unfunded commitments — commitments now frozen for 2026 (April 14 initiatives). No solvency issue.

**Arithmetic correction:** tender share capacity uses the tender price, not the current market price; the original 15% acceptance and 3.1% premium estimates were overstated. The 2.5% buyback illustration also produces 0.64% NAV/share accretion, not 0.8–1.0%. These component calculations are not a total-return model: the value and share count of the remaining holding must also be reconciled before adding discount narrowing, tender proceeds and buyback effects. The historical HVPE scenario returns below have not been rebuilt.

**PEY** — **Price data conflict, flagged:** AIC (July 9) shows £7.28 price / £11.84 NAV = 38.5% discount; the April 30 NAV per the June 12 RNS headline is €13.00/share (content unverified); Investing.com showed €7.08 on June 30 (source reliability low); QuotedData cited a "chronic 28%" discount at the June 18 announcement. - Harvestable spread arithmetic (the reason this leg gets downgraded): elect 100% into realisation shares, get scaled to the 30% cap. That 30% redeems semi-annually at NAV-less-costs over an expected **8 years** at 125bps running fees; PV of that runoff slice at a 12% discount rate with a 40/30/20/10 pacing ≈ **~0.78 × NAV**. The other 70% stays continuation shares at 150bps + incentive; assume the discount stays ~38%: value 0.62 × NAV. Blended: 0.3×0.78 + 0.7×0.62 = **0.67 × NAV vs. 0.61–0.65 paid → only ~3–9% total uplift, realized over years.** Even if continuation shares re-rate to a 30% discount, blended value is 0.72 × NAV → ~+15% total, still multi-year and NAV-integrity-dependent. ## 7. Pre-mortem: the three most likely ways this loses money, and tripwires/kill criteria

1. **The PE marks are fiction (HVPE, PEY).** 86% of HVPE's NAV is on Dec-2025 GP marks. If the 2026 exit environment stays shut and secondaries clear at 80–85 cents, "buying at 74.5 and exiting at 90" becomes "buying at real-NAV 95 and exiting at real-NAV 100" — fine but pedestrian — while sentiment pushes the stated discount wider. *Tripwires:* (a) December-2025 and July-2026 HVPE asset sales' pricing vs carrying value once disclosed — **kill/trim if either printed below ~88% of carry**; (b) Q3 2026 HarbourVest mark revisions vs public-market comps; (c) autumn tender uptake — if far more than ~50% of shares tender, holders are telling you stated NAV is not real.
2. **SEIT's leverage eats the wind-down.** Debt is repaid before equity sees a penny; a 15%+ realization haircut cycle (steel-linked US assets in a downturn, stranded development projects) takes stressed NAV toward 55p and pushes distributions past 2030 — at 36.6p that is a ~10% IRR for distressed-grade risk, and materially worse below 50p. *Tripwires:* next two disposal prints vs carrying value (**kill if slippage >15%**); RCF balance each results release (should fall every half); any further NAV markdown >5% in H1 FY27; Saba stake changes (Saba selling = kill).
3. **Event-window beta and crowding.** EOT is long European equities unhedged for ~2 months; VEIL is long a +46% twelve-month market at all-time highs where 70% of your fellow holders just tried to leave; HVPE is the most crowded activist trade in London (Saba via TRS — swaps can unwind fast, and Saba's GDV behavior shows it will dump and move on after a win *or* a loss). A 10% risk-off move wipes out a year of expected spread capture on the thin legs. *Tripwires:* hedge EOT (short SX5E/SXXP futures) or skip it; cap VEIL at a starter; **watch Saba TRS notifications on HVPE after the tender — if Saba's economic exposure drops below ~5%, expect the discount to re-widen and reassess.*

**US-investor friction (applies to all but GDV, flag prominently):** all five London names are PFICs for a US taxable investor. Practical handling: hold in a taxable account with a **mark-to-market election (Form 8621 per fund, per year)** — gains become ordinary income, no LTCG treatment, admin per position; short holds (<1 yr, e.g., EOT) land as ordinary income under the default regime anyway with no interest charge. UK stamp duty/SDRT of 0.5% applies to purchases of the UK-incorporated plcs (**EOT, SEIT**) but not the Guernsey (HVPE, PEY) or Cayman (VEIL) companies (standard treatment; confirm with broker — unverified for these specific lines). No UK withholding on dividends or CGT on gains for US holders. Net effect: friction argues for concentrating in 2–3 names, not running the six-ticket basket; IRA/tax-deferred accounts avoid the PFIC issue for names your custodian will hold there.

## 8. Verdict & the price/fact that changes it

**PURSUE — but as 2–3 positions, not the basket.** The six-name basket fails its own logic: equal-weighting would put a third of capital into a dead catalyst (GDV) and a diluted one (PEY). The correct construction, in order:

1. **HVPE (core, ~half of the cluster allocation):** 25.5% stated / ~17% haircut-adjusted discount, subject to the July 15 continuation vote (still pending at the research date), a $400M tender ~21% above the current price on the accepted slice, $500M+ of 2026 distributions, and a governance regime now contractually returning 5–10% of NAV annually. The original low-to-mid-teens 12-month return estimate and modest-downside characterization require revision after the §4 arithmetic corrections. *Changes the verdict:* discount closing below ~15% pre-tender (spread gone), the continuation vote failing (take the wind-up instead — likely better), or asset-sale pricing revealed below ~88% of carry (thesis becomes NAV-integrity-limited).
2. **EOT (short-dated arb, sized to taste, hedged):** ~2.1% gross (~1.6% after the assumed 0.5% stamp duty, before other costs) at the 910p research-date price, over ~2 months with a daily-marked liquid NAV. The historical rule requires a hedge and ≥1.5% net return on entry cost. **Arithmetic correction:** with a 929p cash exit, the ceiling before other costs is 929 / (1.005 × 1.015) ≈ **910.7p**; the 928–931p exit range implies ceilings of approximately 909.7–912.7p. At the former 925p threshold, 929 / (925 × 1.005) − 1 ≈ **−0.07%** after stamp duty. These calculated ceilings supersede the fixed buy/pass thresholds and must be lowered for brokerage and hedging costs; the FAV, dividend treatment and final exit proceeds still need confirmation.
3. **SEIT (small, explicitly speculative):** 36.6p vs a stressed ~64p realization value = ~16% multi-year IRR with Saba enforcing pace. Size at ≤1/4 of the HVPE position. *Changes the verdict:* next disposal within 5% of carry at meaningful size → upgrade and add; slippage >15% or a stalled RCF paydown → kill.
- **VEIL — WATCH.** Re-enter on a VN-Index correction of ~15%+ or a discount >13%; the Sep 21 inclusion and tender #3 will still be there. Not at all-time highs with a 10.4% discount.
- **PEY — WATCH.** Read the Q3 circular. Upgrade only if (a) the realisation-class redemption pacing is front-loaded (>40% inside 2 years), or (b) the board adds continuation-class buybacks at wide discounts. And first resolve the price/NAV data conflict.
- **GDV — PASS.** The fact that would change it: Saba (or another activist) re-filing with a credible path around the majority-of-outstanding standard, or the discount blowing past ~15% with the 6% distribution intact.
