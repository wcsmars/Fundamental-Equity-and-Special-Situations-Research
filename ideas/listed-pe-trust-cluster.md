## 1. Situation in three sentences

Three years of rate-shock deratings, UK wealth-manager consolidation, and activist pressure (Saba, AVI) have forced the boards of a cluster of London-listed trusts to legislate their own discounts away — via continuation votes, tenders at 2.5–10% discounts to NAV, realisation share classes, and outright wind-ups — on published timetables running from July 2026 through 2027+. The trade is buying discounts of 5–53% and being paid out at 90–98% of NAV by mechanical corporate actions rather than by market re-rating. The catch, confirmed by this work: GDV has no identified live catalyst and PEY offers only a conditional partial exit, the deepest discount (SEIT) sits behind 75% gearing, and the two private-equity names carry NAVs that are 86%+ two-quarters stale, so the harvestable spread after fees, pro-ration, time, and (for a US buyer) PFIC/stamp friction is far smaller than the headline discounts suggest.

## 2. Who is selling and why (seller-identity analysis — the core of the edge)

The quality of this trade differs name by name precisely because the seller identity differs:

- **HVPE — structural, non-economic sellers; the strongest setup.** The selling since 2022 is UK wealth-manager consolidation (merged firms dumping sub-scale trust lines), the UK cost-disclosure regime that made fund-of-funds look grotesquely expensive on paper (HVPE's 2.17% ongoing includes underlying HarbourVest fund fees), and multi-asset mandates exiting "illiquids" wholesale. The dossier does not establish these holders’ motives or exclude valuation-driven selling. The buyers are informed: Saba disclosed **10.06% combined exposure on July 6, 2026** (reported as mostly total return swaps; separate direct voting rights from financial-instrument exposure in the original notification), a week before the vote; AVI holds 3.1%. Caveat: this also means the trade is *crowded* — you are buying alongside, not ahead of, the activists.
- **PEY — sellers may be partly right.** Same structural LPE fatigue, aggravated by an orphaned EUR quote line on the LSE, sub-scale size (€0.8bn net assets at April 30, 2026), and genuinely poor performance (QuotedData: an ~11% five-year loss, worst tier of the AIC Private Equity sector). When the seller's complaint is manager skill and the "fix" leaves 70%+ of capital with the same manager at 150bps, seller identity gives less edge.
- **EOT — time-and-process arbitrage only.** The base has already rotated to arbitrageurs after the wind-up announcement (shares jumped to a 6.5% discount in May; 47.8% of holders pre-committed support). Today's remaining ~2–3 point spread is compensation for six weeks of completion/beta risk, sold to you by (a) platforms and retail brokers that force-sell rather than process s110 scheme elections, and (b) mandates that cannot hold a delisting/liquidating vehicle. Real but small edge.
- **SEIT — the cleanest forced seller in the cluster.** The June 2026 dividend suspension ("no regular dividends until the RCF is significantly repaid") mechanically evicted income funds and yield-screen retail — holders whose mandate, not their analysis, made them sell (shares fell 19% on the announcement). A 53% discount created by eviction, with Saba at 21.6% policing the wind-down, is the right *shape* of opportunity; the question is asset value and leverage, not seller identity (§4).
- **GDV — the informed money is now the seller.** Saba lost the June 29 vote; the cited June 11 filing reportedly showed a 390,926-share reduction and ~5.35% stake, but predates the vote and cannot establish selling in response to the loss, and Gabelli's majority-of-outstanding voting standard plus insider/preferred voting power make GDV difficult to challenge under the cited voting structure. A post-vote holdings filing is needed to establish whether Saba exited. The failed vote removes the identified catalyst.
- **VEIL — mixed and partly informed.** Frontier-mandate funds must exit before Vietnam leaves the FTSE Frontier index (September 2026), a genuine non-economic seller. But **70.06% of shares outstanding were tendered** into the July 10% tender — the holder base overwhelmingly wants out at 2.5% below NAV after a +46.6% twelve-month VN-Index run to record highs (~1,929). High participation alone does not identify informed selling: a tender above market offers holders a premium while returning unaccepted shares.

## 3. Business quality & moat

These are funds; "quality" = asset quality + NAV credibility + governance.

- **HVPE:** ~$4.4bn (£ equivalent) diversified global buyout/growth fund-of-funds via HarbourVest — a top-decile PE platform with real access moat. Asset quality good; structure (double fee layer) is the permanent defect the discount prices. Governance now shareholder-aligned under duress: no new commitments in 2026, distribution-pool policy and intended annual returns are described in the primary-source correction below; they are not a contractual guaranteed yield.
- **PEY:** Partners Group direct/co-investment book, EUR-denominated. Manager is blue-chip but this vehicle's five-year record is poor; incentive fees survive the restructuring on both classes (125bps on realisation shares vs 150bps continuing). Mediocre.
- **EOT:** Liquid, large/mid-cap listed European equities (Darwall's concentrated book). NAV is daily, real, and verifiable — a liquid listed-equity NAV; prices can still change before realization and other listed portfolios in the cluster also publish daily NAVs. The "business" ends in August.
- **SEIT:** Esoteric energy-efficiency infrastructure (CHP, on-site generation; US steel-linked Primary Energy, etc.). Portfolio generated £84M cash in FY26 (vs £97M prior) and management insists assets "perform in line with expectations," but the equity sits behind consolidated gearing of **83.1% of NAV at March 31, 2026 (74.5% pro-forma post-disposal)** vs a 65% policy limit. Quality of assets: middling and hard to price; quality of balance sheet: poor — that is what 53% is paying you for.
- **GDV:** Perfectly fine diversified US large-cap equity CEF with cheap structural leverage (preferreds) and a 6.1% managed distribution. No moat, no event.
- **VEIL:** $1.4bn+ (£1.05bn mcap) Dragon Capital flagship; best-in-class local research access to Vietnamese banks/property/retail. Underlying is listed, NAV daily and credible. Quality is real; the risk is the asset class's temperature, not the vehicle.

## 7. Pre-mortem: the three most likely ways this loses money, and tripwires/kill criteria

1. **PE marks overstate realizable value (HVPE, PEY).** The cited NAV-date mix is stale and needs checking. At realizations of 80–85% of stated NAV, paying 74.5% equates to ~88–93% of realizable NAV. A tender at 90% of revised NAV would pay ~3,204–3,404p against the 3,315p entry on the accepted slice. **Correction history (September 2026; integrated October 1):** the original 95% real-NAV ratio was overstated and the >50% tender-uptake kill rule conflicted with the assumed 85% uptake. High uptake at a premium is not independent evidence of false marks. The Dec-25 sale's disclosed 6% blended discount is useful calibration, not proof that the remaining book clears at the same price. 2. **SEIT's leverage eats the wind-down.** Debt is repaid before equity sees a penny; a 15%+ realization haircut cycle (steel-linked US assets in a downturn, stranded development projects) takes stressed NAV toward 55p and pushes distributions past 2030 — at 36.6p a 55p terminal receipt in 4.5 years annualizes to ~9.5%, with lower annualization if later; the date and interim payments determine IRR for distressed-grade risk, and materially worse below 50p. *Tripwires:* next two disposal prints vs carrying value (**kill if slippage >15%**); RCF balance each results release (should fall every half); any further NAV markdown >5% in H1 FY27; Saba stake changes (Saba selling = kill).
