## 1. Situation in three sentences

S&P Global completed the 1:1 spin-off of Mobility Global (CARFAX, Polk Automotive Solutions, automotiveMastermind, Market Scan) on July 1, 2026; the stock was deleted from the S&P 500 and added to the S&P **SmallCap 600** effective this morning (July 2), and is trading at **~$20.21 intraday (−4.6% today, 10:51 AM ET quote)** — i.e., we are inside the forced-selling window right now. At ~296M shares that is a **~$5.98B market cap, ~$7.8B EV, ~10.8x LTM adjusted EBITDA ($724M) and a ~5.7–6.6% normalized levered owner-earnings yield** for a business that is >80% subscription, guided to 7.5–10% organic revenue growth and 8–11% EBITDA growth, with CARFAX (47% segment EBITDA margins, genuine network-effect data moat) contributing ~65% of revenue and ~75% of segment EBITDA. The complication versus the backlog entry: the entire data/analytics comp set de-rated hard in the February 2026 AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" (Verisk ~19x vs. 25–29x historical, CCC ~13x, Gartner −50%), so the exit multiple assumption must come down — but even against de-rated comps, today's entry is a discount, and the seller identity is unusually clean.

## 2. Who is selling and why (seller-identity analysis — the core of the edge)

This is close to a textbook Greenblatt setup: the sellers are identifiable, non-economic, and time-concentrated.

**(a) S&P 500 index funds — mechanical, deadline-driven.** The Form 10 itself states that, as of May 15, 2026, **~29% of S&P Global's outstanding stock was held by index funds** tied to the S&P 500 and other indices, and warns verbatim that these funds "will likely be required to sell the shares of our common stock they receive in the Separation" and that "there may not be sufficient buying interest to offset sales." S&P DJI announced (June 26 release) that MBGL joins the **S&P SmallCap 600** (replacing Core Laboratories) **effective prior to the open today, July 2** — meaning S&P 500 trackers had to be out by last night's/this morning's rebalance. Rough sizing: 29% × 296M shares ≈ 86M shares (~$1.7–1.9B at current prices) held by index funds of all stripes; S&P 500 trackers would normally sell their allocated spin shares to realign with the index (exact split within the 29% unverified). Total-market and Russell funds can keep theirs, so call the hard forced supply plausibly **$0.8–1.3B (unverified estimate)**.

**(b) The offsetting buy is structurally small.** The replacement index is the SmallCap 600, whose tracking assets are an order of magnitude smaller than the S&P 500's. A ~$6B company entering the S&P 600 becomes one of its largest members (~0.4–0.5% weight); indexed S&P 600 AUM of roughly $100B+ implies forced *buying* of only ~$300–600M (unverified estimate). Net imbalance on these estimates: **$0.2–1.0B, or ~3–17% of market cap**, without assuming all selling occurs within days. Volume evidence: 5.9M shares traded by 10:51 AM today alone. **Arithmetic correction (September 2026 review):** the estimates above ($0.8–1.3B hard forced supply less ~$0.3–0.6B forced S&P 600 buying) imply a net imbalance of about $0.2–1.0B, or ~3–17% of the ~$5.98B market cap (midpoint ~10%); 10–20% matches the gross hard supply (~13–22%), not the net. The imbalance remains material, and the verdict does not rest on this percentage.

**(c) Large-cap active holders — "too small to care."** SPGI is owned as a ratings/index-oligopoly compounder. MBGL is a ~3–4% stub of the pre-spin position (approx.; $6B vs. ~$160B+ parent, unverified precise ratio), it is auto-exposed, small-cap, and outside most SPGI holders' mandates. The rational portfolio-manager action is to sell without doing the work.

**(d) Optics sellers.** Pro forma GAAP EPS is only **$0.47** (2025) because of **$296M/yr of non-cash IHS-Markit purchase-accounting amortization**, so the stock screens at ~43x P/E while trading at ~11x EV/EBITDA and ~15.1–17.6x estimated owner earnings. Quant/EPS screens will spit this out as "expensive and levered." This is a possible screening disadvantage, not evidence of a guaranteed return. **Correction (September 2026 review):** after the §4 FCF cross-check correction, the owner-earnings multiple is ≈15.1–17.6x.

**(e) Who is NOT selling.** Management moved *into* the spinco: CEO Bill Eager ran CARFAX for 20+ years (CEO since 2021, built Advantage/Car Listings/CARFAX For Life — the core products); Chairman is Joe Hinrichs (CSX CEO, ex-Ford); CFO Matt Calderone (background reportedly Booz Allen — unverified). Converted equity awards preserve intrinsic value (no windfall), and the new incentive plan (S-8 filed July 1; reserve = 14% of shares — large, worth watching) means initial standalone grants will be struck at these depressed post-spin prices. Watch the first Form 4s for grant dates/prices and any open-market buys.

**Price action is consistent with the mechanics, but does not establish causation:** when-issued traded ~$22–23 (June 26–30, investing.com WI line closed $22.05 on Jul 1 with prior close $23.00), first regular-way day (July 1) ranged ~$19–21 and closed ~$20.26–21.19 (sources conflict; unverified exact close), and today — deletion day — it is down another ~4.6%. The 52-week (WI-inclusive) range shows prints from $26.00 down to $18.10 (unverified timing of those prints). The calendar supplies a plausible explanation for some pressure; index-flow estimates and conflicting quotes do not isolate it from changing fundamental expectations.

## 3. Business quality & moat

Two segments (realigned in 2025; per Form 10):

**CARFAX (~65% of revenue; the crown jewel).** 2025 revenue $1,142M, Segment Adjusted EBITDA $536M (**47% margin**, up from 45% in 2023 → 46% in 2024). B2B2C model: dealers subscribe (Advantage, Car Listings, CARFAX For Life) because consumers demand the report; consumers demand the report because the data is unmatched. The data estate is the moat: **38B+ vehicle history records from 177,000+ sources — 92,000+ dealers and service shops, 6,300 police agencies, 36 OEMs — covering ~832M unique VINs with 2,000+ attributes each, plus 13B transaction records** (Form 10). Contributors supply data partly because they also consume it ("symbiotic data partnerships") — a two-sided network effect accumulated over ~40 years that cannot be replicated with capital or with LLMs: the accident/service/title records are proprietary event data, not synthesizable content. 40,000+ dealer subscription customers; ~23M monthly average unique visitors to CARFAX web/apps (2025). Also sells into BIG (banks/insurance/government) verticals. Competitor Experian AutoCheck exists but has far lower consumer mindshare (competitive gap widely accepted but relative share unverified).

**Mobility Business Solutions / "B2B" (~35% of revenue; the weaker half).** 2025 revenue $608M, Segment Adjusted EBITDA $197M (**32% margin — down from 35% in 2024, 34% in 2023**). Polk registration data/forecasting (OEM-facing), automotiveMastermind (dealer predictive marketing), Market Scan (payments/deal data). Note honestly: B2B segment EBITDA was ~flat 2023–2025 ($191M → ~$198M → $197M) on growing revenue — margin erosion, and this is where the AI-disruption and dealer-marketing-cyclicality bear cases concentrate. Competitors: J.D. Power, Cox Automotive, Experian, GlobalData (per Form 10).

**Mix and stickiness.** Subscription = **$1,426M of $1,750M (81.5%)** in 2025; Q1 2026 the same ~82%. Revenue grew 9% in both 2024 and 2025; Q1 2026 +8.3% (CARFAX +8.4%, B2B +8.3%). "Revenue retention is consistently high" per Form 10 (no NRR number disclosed — flag). Capex is only **0.9–1.4% of revenue** — a capital-light royalty on used-car transactions. Overall Adjusted EBITDA margin ~40–41% for three straight years.

**Cyclicality — the honest caveat.** Dealer subscriptions (Advantage/Listings) are tied to dealer counts and marketing budgets; Mastermind is explicitly a marketing-spend product. The 2026 dealer backdrop is stressed: affordability is the #1 dealer concern at record survey levels, 58% of auto borrowers have negative equity (Cox, Feb 2026), tariffs are pushing new-car prices up 2–4%. Offset: used-vehicle activity (CARFAX's engine) typically holds up *better* than new in downturns, and off-lease supply is recovering. But this is not Verisk-grade acyclicality, and B2B's OEM forecasting revenue is exposed to OEM cost-cutting.

Verdict on quality: CARFAX alone is a wide-moat, ~47%-margin, price-making asset that would command a premium multiple as a standalone; consolidated quality is diluted by the B2B segment to "good-plus."

## 7. Pre-mortem: the three most likely ways this loses money, and tripwires/kill criteria

**1. Auto-cycle hit to dealer wallets (most likely).** Affordability is at record-stress levels (58% of borrowers in negative equity, Cox Feb 2026; tariff-driven price increases). Dealer consolidation/failures shrink the subscriber base; Mastermind (marketing spend) gets cut first; OEMs cut Polk forecasting seats. With $111M fixed interest, a 10% EBITDA decline (~$71M) is a ~13.6% owner-earnings decline in the fixed-cost $383M bridge ($71M × 73% / $383M), and the multiple compresses simultaneously.
*Tripwires:* CARFAX dealer customers falling below ~40,000 or declining two consecutive quarters; consolidated organic growth <5% for two straight quarters; B2B segment revenue declining y/y. **Kill: two consecutive quarters of negative organic growth or dealer-count decline >3%.**

**2. AI disruption narrative becomes AI disruption fact.** The moat argument holds for CARFAX's event data (accidents, service, title — not synthesizable), but ~35% of revenue (B2B: forecasting, analytics, marketing intelligence) is partially LLM-commoditizable, and B2B margins are *already* eroding (35%→32%, 2024→2025). If AI-native tools cut into Mastermind renewals or OEMs in-source analytics, the "half-moat" framing fails and the market will price the whole thing at Gartner-style multiples.
*Tripwires:* B2B Segment EBITDA margin <30%; Mastermind growth <10% (management framed >15% as validation); any disclosed loss of a major data contributor (insurer/police-data channel) or an OEM data-sharing dispute. **Kill: CARFAX segment EBITDA margin <44% (would signal the moat itself is leaking).**

**3. Spin-execution and capital-allocation self-harm.** Standalone costs overshoot the $20–25M guide (equity comp "alignment with peers" is an open-ended uplift; 14% plan reserve is large); TSA exit slips beyond 18 months; or management uses "M&A as an accelerant" to do a big, levered, dilutive deal near the 3.5x covenant while the stock is cheap — turning a capital-return story into an empire-building one.
*Tripwires:* standalone cost guidance revised above $30M; net leverage >3.25x; any single acquisition >$500M in year one; buyback start slipping past mid-2027; dividend not declared by the Q3'26 report. **Kill: leverage >3.5x or a transformative acquisition announced before the balance sheet is proven.**

## 8. Verdict & the price/fact that changes it

**PURSUE, conditional on the remaining cash-tax and standalone-cost checks.** At the July 2 snapshot, 10.8x LTM EBITDA offers a plausible index-flow discount, with a modeled **5.7–6.6% owner-earnings yield** and **26–41% bear-case price loss**. The original 1–2% starter and scale-up plan is a historical sizing illustration; full sizing is not justified until the open tax/cash-flow items clear. In the $18s, yield is approximately **6.0–7.4%** across both the $18–19 entry band and the $340–395M earnings range, not a uniform 7%; EV/LTM EBITDA is **9.9–10.3x**, and ≤10x requires price ≤**$18.21** on $1.85B net debt.

**The price that changes it:** above **~$27–28** (≈12.5–13x forward EV/EBITDA) the forced-selling edge is gone and this becomes WATCH — the remaining return is just the business compounding, available elsewhere with less cyclicality. Below **~$17.50**, the estimated yield is 6.6–7.6%, but the same tax, cost and dealer-count checks remain prerequisites to increasing size.

**The fact that changes it:** documented incremental cash taxes absent from the model, or persistent dealer-count/B2B contraction, require a new valuation before sizing. The September 2026 finite-DTL correction is incorporated: a genuinely additional $983M paid evenly over 12 years is worth ~$617M at 8%, or **$2.09/share**, versus $3.32/share undiscounted. Applied once to the corrected $25.34–26.65 base, the sensitivity is **$22.02–24.56**, not a blanket $21 target. The October primary-source check finds historical reversal already in CFO; this sensitivity must not be stacked on cash flows that already capture it. Two consecutive quarters of dealer-count decline or B2B revenue contraction remain the operating reassessment trigger.
