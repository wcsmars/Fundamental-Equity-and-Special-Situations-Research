## 1. Situation in three sentences

On March 27, 2026 a divided Second Circuit panel (2-1, Cabranes dissenting) reversed outright — on the merits under Argentine law, not on procedural or immunity grounds — the $16.1B judgment against Argentina that underpinned Burford's largest asset, and the stock cratered ~45% in a day (from ~$7.70 on March 26 to the low $4s); en banc rehearing has since been denied (mandate issued June 10, 2026), leaving only a long-shot Supreme Court cert petition (deadline requires the rehearing order and any extension) and a multi-year investment-treaty arbitration path. Burford wrote YPF down from $2.6B to $150M (consolidated; $92.5M Burford-only), leaving shareholders' equity of $828M ($3.78/share) against a July 1 close of ~$4.16–4.32 (market cap ~$913–946M). **The backlog thesis is factually wrong at today's price: the stock trades at ~1.24–1.29x ex-YPF GAAP book and ~1.31–1.36x reported tangible book (the latter still includes the residual YPF value), so the YPF optionality is not free — you are paying a premium to a Level 3-marked, 2.9x-levered book whose credibility is precisely the contested question.**

## 2. Who is selling and why (seller-identity analysis)

- **March 27 sellers (the -45% day):** likely included holders of BUR as a YPF judgment-enforcement play; fund identities and flow attribution are unverified. Their thesis (collect on a $16B judgment, aided by the June 30, 2025 turnover order on Argentina's YPF shares ([company filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1714174/000171417425000141/bur-20250701xex991.htm))) died on the spot — the reversal was on the merits with no remand, which is close to the worst possible outcome. Selling was rational, not forced. Burford's own earnings call conceded the market had handicapped reversal odds "in the single digits" walking into the Second Circuit.
- **April–June sellers:** after the Q1 10-Q (May 8) laid out the full damage, Deutsche Bank downgraded to Hold at $5.14 (May 11, PT $5.00) — explicitly *not* about YPF, but because "realizations from the core business over the past year have fallen below the level needed to generate target returns" and the company is "quickly burning through cash" with front-loaded interest/opex ("negative carry"). BofA cut its PT to $5.61 (kept Buy); B. Riley to $7.50 (Buy, values YPF at zero). These analyst concerns support a fundamental explanation for the de-rating; they do not identify the residual seller or exclude simultaneous forced sales.
- **UK/AIM retail and income holders:** BUR is dual-listed on AIM; some UK retail exodus is plausible (unverified — no holdings-flow data obtained), and its importance to current flows is unknown.
- **Who is NOT selling — and not buying:** management had substantial disclosed ownership (Bogart 4.16%, Molot 4.39%, all D&O 8.91% per the April 2026 proxy), which is not evidence of a contractual lock-up, and Bogart/Molot put ~$4.3M into shares via the deferred-comp plan on March 5, 2026 — *before* the ruling, at roughly double today's price. Post-crash Form 4s (March 30, June 17) show only compensation-related transactions (vestings, tax withholdings at $7.70 and $4.64). **The filings cited here do not establish meaningful open-market buying at $4; no complete transaction inventory is retained, so absence is not conclusively verified.** Institutions: Orbis 8.2%, Conifer 5.55%, BlackRock 5.18% (as of March 2026 proxy; post-crash positioning unverified).
- **Verdict on the edge:** the claimed edge ("market panic-priced the stock below ex-YPF NAV") does not survive contact with the numbers. Price ≈ 124–129% of ex-YPF book using the stated $4.16–4.32 price range. The market is paying a premium to that adjusted book value. Whatever edge exists here is an *analytical* disagreement about Level 3 mark conservatism and future realization pace — not a seller-identity dislocation.

## 3. Business quality & moat

- **The good:** Burford is the scale player in litigation finance — 237 active balance-sheet assets across ~900 cases, $2.2B Burford-only portfolio fair value, $3.1B AUM in managed funds (incl. BOF-C, the sovereign-wealth-fund partnership), >$6B cumulative group-wide cash realizations since inception, and a concluded-portfolio track record of **83% ROIC / 26% IRR** on $3.77B of cumulative Burford-only realizations (per FY2025 10-K). Origination relationships with major law firms, proprietary case data, and underwriting infrastructure are genuine, hard-to-replicate assets. Case outcomes differ from market drivers, but this note has not measured return correlation; funding, duration and valuation still carry market risk.
- **The bad:** this is not a compounding machine with a toll-booth moat; it is a levered, lumpy, opaque investment portfolio. Revenue is dominated by non-cash fair-value movement on Level 3 assets (the YPF episode is the canonical demonstration: $2.4B of book value vaporized by one opinion). Duration is stretching (weighted average life of deployed capital rose to 3.3 yrs), asset-management *fee* income is de minimis ($0.3M in Q1 2026 — the AM economics flow through consolidated profit shares), and the LTI/carried-interest comp plan gives management a large cut of portfolio upside ahead of shareholders (ISS recommended against say-on-pay in 2025; it passed in 2026 after outreach).
- **Competitive backdrop:** capital has flooded litigation finance since 2019; pricing pressure on new commitments is a live question (unverified). New commitments are still growing ($133M definitive in Q1 2026, +25% vs. prior-year averages; $1.3B unfunded definitive commitments — which are funding commitments, not already-recognized debt, but create liquidity obligations as drawn).
- **Muddy Waters (Aug 2019) — do the criticisms still bite?** MW's core claims: (i) aggressive/manipulable Level 3 marks drive reported earnings (Enron mark-to-model analogy); (ii) ROIC/IRR metrics massaged; (iii) governance — the CFO was CEO Bogart's wife; (iv) "arguably insolvent." Since then Burford NYSE-listed (2020), became an SEC registrant with 10-K/10-Q discipline, replaced the CFO (now Jordan Licht; Elizabeth O'Connell remains an executive officer per 2026 Form 4s), and rebuilt its fair-value methodology after a 2021–2023 SEC comment-letter process. **Half-vindicated:** YPF proved that fair-value earnings can be phantom (MW's central point), but note the write-down worked as designed — marks went down as well as up, third parties absorbed $0.8B of the loss, and the *residual* book is now marked at only a 22% premium to deployed cost (management's own number) vs. 83% historically realized ROIC — the absolute mark-to-cost premium is lower, without proving that remaining marks are conservative. The 2019 "insolvency" claim was wrong; liquidity held. **Correction (September 2026 review):** the 22% is management's figure, on a basis this dossier does not reconcile; the §4 Burford-only figures give 2,230.0 / 1,868.1 ≈ 1.19x deployed cost (a ~19% premium, including the $92.5M residual YPF asset), the multiple used elsewhere in this dossier. **Methodology correction (October 1, 2026):** the 1.19x mark is a present fair value of ongoing assets; 1.83x is an undiscounted realized multiple for a different, concluded cohort. Remaining cost commitments, time value, selection of faster resolutions and vintage mix prevent treating that gap as unrecognized profit or proof of mark conservatism.

## 7. Pre-mortem: three most likely ways this loses money, and tripwires

1. **Slow-bleed negative carry (most likely).** Realizations stay ~$400M/yr while fixed costs and dividends pressure liquidity; the assumed book erosion requires a separate fair-value/retained-earnings bridge; duration keeps extending; stock de-rates from 1.1x to 0.7x on a shrinking base → $2.00–2.50. **Arithmetic correction (September 2026 review):** ≈$1.55–2.01 on the §5 bear inputs (see the §5 Bear correction). *Tripwires:* TTM Burford-only realizations < $350M; cash + marketables < $450M without a corresponding deployment cut; dividend maintained while book shrinks (denial signal); WAL of deployed capital > 3.6 yrs.
2. **Marks credibility break.** As 2026–27 trials conclude, realized proceeds come in *below* prior carrying values, proving the 1.19x-cost marks optimistic and destroying the only valuation anchor; KPMG qualifications or renewed SEC comment letters compound it. *Tripwires:* any quarter with net realized losses on concluded core assets; gross realized losses >20% of like-for-like segment realizations (FY25: $53.9M/$443.854M ≈12.1%); discount-rate assumption cuts used to prop marks; auditor/SEC noise.
3. **Refinancing squeeze.** Post-YPF, HY investors re-price litigation-finance paper; the $400M 2028s can't be refinanced below ~10% (the company already paid 8.50% in Jan 2026 *with* the YPF judgment intact), forcing asset sales at discounts to marks — a public, real-time invalidation of NAV. *Tripwires:* 2028/2031 notes trading at yields > 9.5–10%; any rating downgrade (current ratings unverified); secondary portfolio sales announced at haircuts to carrying value; restricted-payment basket exhaustion language expanding in 10-Qs.

Kill criteria for any position: two of the three tripwire clusters firing simultaneously, or realized-vs-mark evidence turning negative for two consecutive quarters.

## 8. Verdict & the price/fact that changes it

**WATCH.** The July price of $4.16–4.32 exceeds simple ex-YPF GAAP book (~1.24–1.29x); the original free-option claim is unsupported. Most capital provision assets are Level 3, but the entire balance sheet is not: it also contains cash, marketable securities and other assets. Debt/attributable equity is ~2.9x. **October 1, 2026 review:** the corrected realization and cash-receipt definitions still leave weak pre-tax fixed-charge coverage under the draft's assumptions, without proving an annual GAAP book decline. The gap between current fair value and historical realized ROIC does not establish conservative marks. YPF success probabilities, conditional recoveries and legal deadlines are unverified; the old $0.30–0.70/share option estimate and $4–5 base case are withdrawn as decision-ready valuations. Observe actual cash receipts, case outcomes versus prior marks and refinancing capacity before committing capital.

**Re-underwriting triggers (no automatic PURSUE until cash-flow and valuation gaps are resolved):**
- **Price:** below ~$2.75 (≈0.73x reported BVPS and ≈0.87x reported TBVPS) absent worsening evidence. The corrected partial enterprise bridge is ($2.75 × 219.07M + $2,401.8M + $55M − $740.266M − $180.041M) / $1,868.1M ≈ **1.145x deployed cost**, not 0.9x. This is an alert for valuation work, not proof of free platform/option value;
- **Fact:** two quarters with adjusted Burford-only realizations annualizing >$550M and conclusions at/above prior marks, accompanied by cash collection. At 83% ROIC, $550M gives ~$249M gains against $267M net fixed charges, so this threshold alone is not break-even;
- **Fact:** formal treaty claims and credible settlement signals warrant revisiting the probability tree, but establish no hard floor before enforceable, collectible recovery.

**What changes it to PASS permanently:** realized-vs-marks evidence turning negative, a dividend maintained into a liquidity squeeze, or 2028 refinancing at distressed terms; these are research stop conditions, not pre-authorized trades.
