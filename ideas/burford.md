# Burford Capital (NYSE/LSE AIM: BUR) — Deep Dive
*Date: 2026-07-02 · Status at research date: WATCH*

> Historical research draft, July 2026. Prices, events and position-sizing examples refer to the dates in this note. Source claims have not been revalidated for this edition; open verification items remain.

## 1. Situation in three sentences

On March 27, 2026 a divided Second Circuit panel (2-1, Cabranes dissenting) reversed outright — on the merits under Argentine law, not on procedural or immunity grounds — the $16.1B judgment against Argentina that underpinned Burford's largest asset, and the stock cratered ~45% in a day (from ~$7.70 on March 26 to the low $4s); en banc rehearing has since been denied (mandate issued June 10, 2026), leaving only a long-shot Supreme Court cert petition (due ~September 2026) and a multi-year investment-treaty arbitration path. Burford wrote YPF down from $2.6B to $150M (consolidated; $92.5M Burford-only), leaving shareholders' equity of $828M ($3.78/share) against a July 1 close of ~$4.16–4.32 (market cap ~$913–946M). **The backlog thesis is factually wrong at today's price: the stock trades at ~1.24–1.29x ex-YPF GAAP book and ~1.31–1.36x reported tangible book (the latter still includes the residual YPF value), so the YPF optionality is not free — you are paying a premium to a Level 3-marked, 2.9x-levered book whose credibility is precisely the contested question.**

## 2. Who is selling and why (seller-identity analysis)

- **March 27 sellers (the -45% day):** event-driven and special-situation funds who owned BUR as a YPF judgment-enforcement play. Their thesis (collect on a $16B judgment, aided by the 2024 turnover order on Argentina's YPF shares) died on the spot — the reversal was on the merits with no remand, which is close to the worst possible outcome. Selling was rational, not forced. Burford's own earnings call conceded the market had handicapped reversal odds "in the single digits" walking into the Second Circuit.
- **April–June sellers:** after the Q1 10-Q (May 8) laid out the full damage, Deutsche Bank downgraded to Hold at $5.14 (May 11, PT $5.00) — explicitly *not* about YPF, but because "realizations from the core business over the past year have fallen below the level needed to generate target returns" and the company is "quickly burning through cash" with front-loaded interest/opex ("negative carry"). BofA cut its PT to $5.61 (kept Buy); B. Riley to $7.50 (Buy, values YPF at zero). The residual seller is therefore **informed, fundamentals-driven skepticism about the ex-YPF business**, not indiscriminate capitulation.
- **UK/AIM retail and income holders:** BUR is dual-listed on AIM; some UK retail exodus is plausible (unverified — no holdings-flow data obtained), but three months post-event this is no longer the marginal seller.
- **Who is NOT selling — and not buying:** management (Bogart 4.16%, Molot 4.39%, all D&O 8.91% per the April 2026 proxy) is locked in, and Bogart/Molot put ~$4.3M into shares via the deferred-comp plan on March 5, 2026 — *before* the ruling, at roughly double today's price. Post-crash Form 4s (March 30, June 17) show only compensation-related transactions (vestings, tax withholdings at $7.70 and $4.64). **There has been no meaningful open-market insider buying at $4 — a tell worth respecting.** Institutions: Orbis 8.2%, Conifer 5.55%, BlackRock 5.18% (as of March 2026 proxy; post-crash positioning unverified).
- **Verdict on the edge:** the claimed edge ("market panic-priced the stock below ex-YPF NAV") does not survive contact with the numbers. Price ≈ 124–129% of ex-YPF book using the stated $4.16–4.32 price range. The market is paying a premium to that adjusted book value. Whatever edge exists here is an *analytical* disagreement about Level 3 mark conservatism and future realization pace — not a seller-identity dislocation.

## 3. Business quality & moat

- **The good:** Burford is the scale player in litigation finance — 237 active balance-sheet assets across ~900 cases, $2.2B Burford-only portfolio fair value, $3.1B AUM in managed funds (incl. BOF-C, the sovereign-wealth-fund partnership), >$6B cumulative group-wide cash realizations since inception, and a concluded-portfolio track record of **83% ROIC / 26% IRR** on $3.77B of cumulative Burford-only realizations (per FY2025 10-K). Origination relationships with major law firms, proprietary case data, and underwriting infrastructure are genuine, hard-to-replicate assets. Returns are largely uncorrelated to markets.
- **The bad:** this is not a compounding machine with a toll-booth moat; it is a levered, lumpy, opaque investment portfolio. Revenue is dominated by non-cash fair-value movement on Level 3 assets (the YPF episode is the canonical demonstration: $2.4B of book value vaporized by one opinion). Duration is stretching (weighted average life of deployed capital rose to 3.3 yrs), asset-management *fee* income is de minimis ($0.3M in Q1 2026 — the AM economics flow through consolidated profit shares), and the LTI/carried-interest comp plan gives management a large cut of portfolio upside ahead of shareholders (ISS recommended against say-on-pay in 2025; it passed in 2026 after outreach).
- **Competitive backdrop:** capital has flooded litigation finance since 2019; pricing pressure on new commitments is a live question (unverified). New commitments are still growing ($133M definitive in Q1 2026, +25% vs. prior-year averages; $1.3B unfunded definitive commitments — which are also a *liability* in a liquidity crunch).
- **Muddy Waters (Aug 2019) — do the criticisms still bite?** MW's core claims: (i) aggressive/manipulable Level 3 marks drive reported earnings (Enron mark-to-model analogy); (ii) ROIC/IRR metrics massaged; (iii) governance — the CFO was CEO Bogart's wife; (iv) "arguably insolvent." Since then Burford NYSE-listed (2020), became an SEC registrant with 10-K/10-Q discipline, replaced the CFO (now Jordan Licht; Elizabeth O'Connell remains an executive officer per 2026 Form 4s), and rebuilt its fair-value methodology after a 2021–2023 SEC comment-letter process. **Half-vindicated:** YPF proved that fair-value earnings can be phantom (MW's central point), but note the write-down worked as designed — marks went down as well as up, third parties absorbed $0.8B of the loss, and the *residual* book is now marked at only a 22% premium to deployed cost (management's own number) vs. 83% historically realized ROIC — i.e., today's marks are the least aggressive in years. The 2019 "insolvency" claim was wrong; liquidity held.

At $600M/yr of realizations and 83% ROIC, the gain component is $600M × 83/183 ≈ $272M; owner earnings on the stated assumptions are ~$272M + $30M − $105M − $192M ≈ **$5M/yr**.

The original $150–250M owner-earnings estimate at $600M realizations overstated gains; that earnings range would require approximately $920M–$1.14B annual realizations at 83% ROIC under the same income and expense assumptions, before any additional taxes or costs. The historical bull valuation below needs a new cash-flow bridge. Book multiples below also distinguish reported equity from equity excluding the $92.5M residual YPF asset; this is simple subtraction, with no associated tax or liability adjustments modeled.

## 5. Valuation: what the price implies, and intrinsic-value range

At $4.32 (July 2, 2026; market cap $946M):

- **Reported P/B = 1.14x** ($3.78 BVPS); **reported P/TBV = 1.36x** ($3.17); both include residual YPF value. Subtracting $92.5M from attributable equity gives ex-YPF equity of $735.4M, **BVPS ~$3.36 and P/B ~1.29x**. Also subtracting $134.0M goodwill gives **ex-YPF TBVPS ~$2.75 and P/TBV ~1.57x**.
- Enterprise math: 946 (equity) + 2,402 (debt) + 55 (accrued interest) − 755 (cash) − 180 (settlement receivables) ≈ **$2.47B** paid for a Burford-only portfolio carried at $2.23B plus the AM platform → **~1.11x carrying value and ~1.32x deployed cost.** This bridge does not separately allocate value to the AM platform or residual YPF asset and does not establish that either is free.
- **What the price implies:** the portfolio realizes at roughly its marks (≈1.2x cost), i.e., far below the 83% historical concluded ROIC — or alternatively that historical ROIC persists but negative carry, comp leakage (carry plan), and duration consume most of the spread. Either is a defensible reading; that is the problem.

**Intrinsic value range (per share, 3–5 yr view):**

- **Bear ($2.00–2.50):** realizations stagnate ≤$400M/yr; fixed charges erode book ~5–8%/yr; marks prove modestly inflated as cases conclude; 2028 refi at punitive rates forces portfolio runoff; market prices 0.7x a shrinking book. YPF arbitration worth ~$0.
- **Base ($4.00–5.00):** portfolio realizes near marks with modest excess (realized ROIC 50–70%), covering carry; book flat-to-slightly-growing; 0.9–1.1x book plus $0.30–0.70 probability-weighted YPF arbitration value (my estimate: ~25–35% chance of an eventual Argentina recovery on a fresh treaty award years out; Burford-only PV in success ~$400–700M ≈ $1.80–3.20/share, heavily discounted for time (5–10 yrs), annulment and enforcement risk — all unverified judgment).
- **Bull ($8.00–10.00; historical scenario, not substantiated by the corrected cash-flow arithmetic):** the original scenario assumed $600M+/yr realizations at ~80% ROIC, low-teens book growth to ~$6.50–7.00 by 2029–30, a ~1.2x book multiple, and $1.50–2.00 from a YPF treaty settlement. At exactly $600M and 80% ROIC, the stated income/expense bridge is approximately break-even; substantially higher realizations or different economics are needed to support the assumed book growth and valuation.

The distribution is wide and the base case is approximately today's price — which is what "no edge" looks like.

## 7. Pre-mortem: three most likely ways this loses money, and tripwires

1. **Slow-bleed negative carry (most likely).** Realizations stay ~$400M/yr while ~$192M interest + ~$105M cash opex + $27M dividend grind book value down; duration keeps extending; stock de-rates from 1.1x to 0.7x on a shrinking base → $2.00–2.50. *Tripwires:* TTM Burford-only realizations < $350M; cash + marketables < $450M without a corresponding deployment cut; dividend maintained while book shrinks (denial signal); WAL of deployed capital > 3.6 yrs.
2. **Marks credibility break.** As 2026–27 trials conclude, realized proceeds come in *below* prior carrying values, proving the 1.19x-cost marks optimistic and destroying the only valuation anchor; KPMG qualifications or renewed SEC comment letters compound it. *Tripwires:* any quarter with net realized losses on concluded core assets; gross realized losses > 20% of realizations (FY2025: $53.9M/$435.5M ≈ 12%); discount-rate assumption cuts used to prop marks; auditor/SEC noise.
3. **Refinancing squeeze.** Post-YPF, HY investors re-price litigation-finance paper; the $400M 2028s can't be refinanced below ~10% (the company already paid 8.50% in Jan 2026 *with* the YPF judgment intact), forcing asset sales at discounts to marks — a public, real-time invalidation of NAV. *Tripwires:* 2028/2031 notes trading at yields > 9.5–10%; any rating downgrade (current ratings unverified); secondary portfolio sales announced at haircuts to carrying value; restricted-payment basket exhaustion language expanding in 10-Qs.

Kill criteria for any position: two of the three tripwire clusters firing simultaneously, or realized-vs-mark evidence turning negative for two consecutive quarters.

## 8. Verdict & the price/fact that changes it

**WATCH.** The backlog entry's core claim — "market values the remaining portfolio at or below ex-YPF NAV, so YPF is a free option" — is empirically false at $4.16–4.32: the stock trades at ~1.24–1.29x ex-YPF book and ~1.31–1.36x reported tangible book (including residual YPF value), on a book that is 100% Level 3, levered ~2.9x debt/attributable-equity, and currently running cash-flow-negative against fixed charges. The en banc denial (June 2026) and near-hopeless cert odds mean the US-court path is effectively dead; the treaty-arbitration path is real but is a 5–10 year, self-funded lottery ticket against a sovereign, worth perhaps $0.30–0.70/share probability-weighted (my estimate, unverified). Meanwhile the honest bull case — conservative residual marks (1.19x cost vs. 1.83x historically realized) plus an accelerating trial calendar — is plausible but is exactly what the next 2–4 quarters of realization data will prove or disprove for free. There is no seller-identity edge and no margin of safety at 1.1x contested book; there is also no case for shorting a book this conservatively re-marked with 8.9% insider ownership. A correct grade here is patience.

**What changes it to PURSUE:**
- **Price:** below ~$2.75 (≈0.73x reported BVPS, ≈0.87x reported TBVPS) absent new negative realization data — a historical threshold for re-underwriting. **Arithmetic correction:** at $2.75, the §5 enterprise-value bridge gives (2.75 × 219.07 + 2,402 + 55 − 755 − 180) / 1,868.1 ≈ **1.14x deployed cost**, not 0.9x. This does not establish that the platform, unrealized gains or arbitration option are free; the price trigger needs support from cash-realization and valuation work; or
- **Fact:** two consecutive quarters with Burford-only realizations annualizing > $550M *and* realized proceeds at or above prior carrying values (marks validated) — then re-underwrite valuation against demonstrated cash earnings; >$550M realizations alone does not establish fixed-charge coverage or make the reported-book multiple cheap; or
- **Fact:** treaty arbitrations formally registered *plus* credible Argentina settlement signals (post-cert-denial), which would put a hard floor under the option value.

**What changes it to PASS permanently:** realized-vs-marks evidence turning negative, a dividend maintained into a liquidity squeeze, or 2028 refinancing at distressed terms.
