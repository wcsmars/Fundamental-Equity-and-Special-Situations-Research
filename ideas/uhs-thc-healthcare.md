# Universal Health Services (UHS) & Tenet Healthcare (THC) — Deep Dive
*Date: 2026-07 · Status at research date: PURSUE (UHS leg) / WATCH (THC leg)*

> Historical research draft, July 2026. Prices, events and position-sizing examples refer to the dates in this note. Source claims have not been revalidated for this edition; open verification items remain.

*Paired dossier: the "secular demand priced as cyclical" healthcare thesis. Prices as of ~July 10–13, 2026: UHS ~$155.57, THC ~$204.25.*

---

## 1. Situation in three sentences

The December 31, 2025 expiration of enhanced ACA subsidies (effectuated exchange enrollment fell ~13%, from 22.1M to 19.2M by Feb 2026) and the OBBBA's $900B+/10yr Medicaid cuts caused a sector-wide derating of for-profit hospitals in late 2025 — but the two names have since diverged sharply: THC re-rated back to ~11.6x forward EPS (+19% over 12 months) after guiding to EBITDA *growth* through a $250M ACA hit, while UHS sits at ~6.5x forward EPS and 5.4x EV/EBITDA, near a decade-low multiple, down ~37% from its 52-week high of $246. The market is not actually punishing UHS for the exchange cliff (UHS has the *least* exchange exposure of the three majors, a ~$75M/2.8%-of-EBITDA headwind vs ~5% at THC and HCA) — it is punishing UHS for having the *most* concentrated Medicaid supplemental/directed-payment exposure (~$1.3B net in 2025, roughly half of EBITDA) plus a behavioral-health litigation tail, and treating both as a permanent earnings reset. The work below shows the backlog's clean "secular vs cyclical" framing is half-right: the mispricing is real at UHS (bear case ≈ current price), but the behavioral segment's earnings are partly policy-manufactured, so this is a cheap-with-hair statistical buy to be sized accordingly, not a compounder; THC is the better business but the market has already closed most of that gap.

## 2. Who is selling and why (seller-identity analysis)

**The tape.** UHS: 52-week range $140.08–$246.33, now ~$156, −14.8% over 12 months, trailing P/E 6.5 vs a 10-year average roughly twice that (one screen puts the current P/E ~55% below its 10-year mean). THC: ~$204, **+19.2%** over 12 months, above its 200-DMA ($199). HCA: recovered to ~14x forward EPS after a Q1 wobble (guides FY26 EPS $29.10–31.50). So "the pair derated" is stale — **only UHS is still derated.** Trefis (July 8, 2026) frames it: market pays ~7.9x operating profit for HCA vs ~4.8x for UHS, the widest gap in years.

**Identifiable seller cohorts at UHS, in rough order of importance:**

1. **Policy-risk avoiders discounting the supplemental-payment cliff where it is most concentrated.** UHS management said it expected to **net ~$1.3B from state supplemental payment programs across 2025** (Philadelphia Inquirer / earnings commentary; behavioral facilities are ~60% of that supplemental Medicaid revenue). Against TTM EBITDA of ~$2.67B, that is ~49% of EBITDA — the highest concentration among the three majors (THC: $1.1–1.2B *gross* supplemental revenue on $4.57B EBITDA; HCA far lower as a share). OBBBA phases provider taxes in expansion states from 6% to 3.5% (0.5pp/yr, FFY2028–2032) and caps state-directed payments at 100% of Medicare rates (110% non-expansion) with phase-down from Jan 1, 2028. A seller who cannot underwrite state-by-state DPP renewals simply exits — and the entire long-only healthcare complex has been told by consultants that provider-tax-financed earnings are "lower quality." This selling is rational risk-avoidance, not a differentiated fundamental view: the company-level bear estimate of the OBBBA hit is **$360–400M/yr by 2032** (cited by bears via Simply Wall St; consistent with grandfathering of Medicare-rate-capped SDPs), i.e., ~15% of EBITDA phased over six years, not 49% overnight.
2. **Guidance-credibility sellers.** UHS missed Q4 2025 (stock −11% on the Feb 25–26 print despite *raising* 2026 guidance), then beat Q1 2026 (adj EPS $5.62, +16% y/y) yet was down ~21.5% in the weeks after reporting (Barchart Q1 teardown) because the reaffirmed FY26 guide requires an H2 volume/pricing ramp (weather/flu cost ~200bps of Q1 volumes; Cedar Hill D.C. ramp "more back-end loaded than originally contemplated"). After the 2025 "volumes shortfall" year, the market is applying a show-me discount to a management team that has historically sandbagged. This cohort sells first and re-underwrites at Q2 (July 27).
3. **Headline/quality-screen sellers on the behavioral litigation tail.** The $535M Pavilion verdict (reduced to $180M, settled May 2025), the $360M Cumberland verdict, plaintiff-bar tallies of "at least $895M" in ordered damages, and active mass-tort recruiting (TorHoerman et al., Illinois facilities) make UHS un-ownable for ESG-screened and headline-sensitive mandates regardless of price. These sellers are structurally price-insensitive — classic source of edge, but the underlying risk is real (see §7).
4. **Read-across sellers from Acadia.** ACHC — the pure-play behavioral comp — took a large goodwill impairment, tumbled ~22% on its Q1 2026 print, and guided supplemental payments down. Generalists sold "behavioral health" as a category; UHS's behavioral franchise (much better run, hospital-based, JV-partnered) got the comp multiple anyway.
5. **No-catalyst value abstainers.** Dual-class structure — the Miller family (founder Alan B. Miller, chairman; son Marc Miller, CEO) holds voting control via Class A/C shares — means no activist path and no takeout premium. Value buyers who need a control catalyst pass, leaving the buyback as the only marginal bid.

**Who is buying:** the company itself — 4.65M shares repurchased in 2025 for $899M (avg $193.38 — note: *above* today's price), 675K more in Q1 2026 for $127M, a stated 2026 floor of $800–900M (≈ **9% of the $9.4B market cap per year**), $1.3B authorization remaining at 3/31/26. Share count −5.8% y/y and −~19% over four years (unverified cumulative figure). No meaningful insider open-market buying in 2026 (a Form 4 check shows small sales) — a modest negative signal worth watching.

**THC seller identity, briefly:** the sellers left in Q4 2025 on the same ACA fears; they were replaced after the Feb 11 guide showed FY26 EBITDA of $4.485–4.785B (midpoint *above* 2025's $4.566B despite the $250M subsidy hit) and after Tenet completed its $1.83B/10.9M-share buyback program in April. At +19% y/y and 11.6x forward, the seller-exhaustion edge at THC has largely been harvested. What remains is a "2027 second cliff" overhang (see §7), which is exactly what would recreate the entry.

**Honest check — do the sellers know something?** Partially, yes: (a) UHS acute same-facility adjusted admissions were *flat* in Q1 2026; growth was all price/acuity (+6.3% rev/adj admission); (b) uninsured admissions +16% y/y in Q1 as exchange patients (−15% admissions) fell off; (c) half of EBITDA rides on state programs Congress just voted to shrink. The sellers' error is treating a 6-year, partially-grandfathered glide path plus a mid-single-digit exchange headwind as a permanent, immediate ~40–50% earnings haircut — while ignoring that the buyback compounds at these prices.

## 3. Business quality & moat

**UHS** (market cap $9.4B, EV $14.4B): two businesses.
- **Behavioral health (43% of 2025 consolidated revenue; ~$7.4B):** largest US behavioral operator — 346 inpatient behavioral facilities (182 US, 164 international/UK) + 119 outpatient, ~24,400 US behavioral beds (backlog figure, order-of-magnitude consistent with facility count; *unverified exact count*). Moat: (1) certificates of need and community opposition make new psych beds genuinely hard to add; (2) UHS is the JV partner of choice for nonprofit systems offloading psych units (e.g., the new 144-bed Pennsylvania JV hospital); (3) demand is structurally growing — same-facility behavioral revenue +7.3% in Q1 2026, revenue per adjusted patient day +5.8%, management: "demand remains strong." Talkspace acquisition ($835M cash, closes Q3 2026, ~$229M 2025 revenue, 6,000 clinicians) bolts a national virtual front door onto the bed network — strategically sound, modest size. Caveats: ~60% of UHS's supplemental Medicaid revenue sits in this segment, so reported behavioral economics overstate unsubsidized economics; UK unit (~$1.0B revenue) is a lower-quality NHS-dependent business; and the segment generates the litigation tail.
- **Acute care (57% of revenue; 29 US hospitals + 35 FSEDs):** concentrated in growth markets (Las Vegas, Riverside CA, DC, Florida, Texas). Locally dense, decent share, but a structurally middling business: flat Q1 volumes, wage growth still 6–7%, and heavy growth capex (three de novo/replacement openings in 2026, first-year losses).
- Balance sheet quality is high (≈1.7x net debt/EBITDA, ~2.0x post-Talkspace). Governance: family-controlled; historically disciplined capital allocators, but no external check.

**THC** (market cap $17.6B, EV $27.8B): a different animal post-transformation.
- **USPI (ambulatory):** 541 ASCs (407 consolidated) + 26 surgical hospitals in 37 states — the #1 ASC platform. Q1 2026: revenue $1.32B (+10.6%), EBITDA $484M (+6.1%, ~37% margin), same-facility revenue +5.3% on rev/case +5.6% (cases −0.3%), double-digit growth in outpatient total joints. Q1 was 22% of the ambulatory full-year EBITDA guide, implying **~$2.2B FY26 USPI EBITDA ≈ ~47% of consolidated** — THC is now (just about) majority-ambulatory by EBITDA on a run-rate basis. Moat: physician-partnership model (equity alignment locks in surgeon supply), payer preference for the lowest-cost surgical site, and the secular migration of joints/spine/cardiac to ASCs. This is a genuinely good business with a 10+ year runway.
- **Hospital segment (49-ish hospitals; *count unverified*):** Q1 EBITDA $678M, down 4% y/y on exchange-driven payer-mix degradation and a $40M prior-year supplemental item. Adequately run (21%+ consolidated margins), but this is the cyclical/policy-exposed half.
- Balance sheet: $2.97B cash, $13.2B debt, 2.24x net leverage — transformed from the near-distressed Tenet of the 2010s. Big caveat on headline FCF: consolidated FCF (~$3.3B TTM) includes the USPI minority partners' share; distributions to NCI (~$0.7–0.8B/yr, *unverified*) must be netted for shareholder FCF (~$2.3–2.5B, still ~13% of market cap).

**Comparative quality verdict:** THC > UHS. USPI is the single best asset across the three majors; UHS behavioral is a good franchise wrapped in policy dependence and tort risk.

## 5. Valuation: what the price implies, and intrinsic-value ranges

**What UHS at $156 implies:** 6.6x guided EPS / 5.4x EBITDA implies the market believes some combination of (a) most of the $1.3B supplemental stream disappears, (b) behavioral growth stalls at Acadia-like levels, (c) a tort judgment large enough to matter (>$1B uninsured), with zero credit for the 9%/yr buyback. Reverse-engineered: at a normal 12x multiple, the price implies sustainable EPS of only ~$13 — i.e., a ~45% permanent earnings haircut vs guidance. **Correction:** §4 models partial supplemental-payment losses, including a loss of half the $1.3B stream; it does not quantify a full unwind or establish that the price-implied haircut would cover one.

## 7. Pre-mortem: the three most likely ways this loses money

**1. The supplemental-payment cliff is bigger and faster than the glide path (primarily UHS).**
If state budgets crack (recession, expiration of grandfathered preprints) or CMS rejects renewals, the hit is not $360–400M-by-2032 but a chunk of the full ~$1.3B net, front-loaded. At $650M lost, UHS EPS falls ~$8/share pre-buyback — the 6.6x becomes 10x on a shrinking base and the stock has no floor argument.
*Tripwires:* CMS preprint rejections or renegotiations (esp. Nevada, Texas, D.C., Florida, California); UHS quarterly net-supplemental disclosure falling >$50M below the ~$1.3B run rate for two consecutive quarters; state legislative sessions (spring 2027) cutting behavioral SDP rates. *Kill criterion:* announced/enacted reductions exceeding ~$150M/yr of net benefit ahead of the OBBBA schedule → exit, thesis broken.

**2. The behavioral litigation tail becomes a mass tort (UHS only).**
Pavilion ($535M→$180M→settled), Cumberland ($360M), plaintiff tallies ≥$895M, and organized recruiting across Illinois facilities. The risk is not any single verdict (self-insurance + excess coverage absorb a lot — reserve adequacy *unverified*) but a pattern: punitive damages are frequently uninsurable, and a consolidated/serial docket would both drain cash and force defensive operational changes (lower census, higher staffing) that permanently compress behavioral margins — the Acadia playbook.
*Tripwires:* any new verdict >$100M; disclosure of materially increased self-insurance reserves in a 10-Q; DOJ re-engagement (echo of the 2020 $122M settlement); admission-practice consent decrees. *Kill criterion:* two additional nine-figure verdicts or an MDL certification → the discount is validated; exit.

**3. Payer-mix decay outruns pricing (both; the 2027 "second cliff").**
2026's 13% effectuated-enrollment decline was round one; 2027 brings +14% median premiums and NBPP rules cutting another 1.2–2M enrollees, while Medicaid work requirements start pushing churn from Jan 2027. UHS Q1 already shows the shape: exchange admissions −15%, uninsured +16%. THC's $250M guide covers 2026 only — the cumulative 2027 exchange hit could approach another $150–250M against the hospital segment, and this time consensus is not braced for it. Meanwhile acute volumes are flat and wages still grow 6–7%: negative operating leverage.
*Tripwires:* UHS Q2/Q3 uncompensated-care and acute rev/adj-admission growth (<3% = trouble); THC hospital-segment EBITDA down >5% y/y for two quarters; 2027 OEP signup data (Dec 2026–Jan 2027). *Kill criteria:* UHS acute same-facility EBITDA declining y/y for two consecutive quarters, or Senate compromise definitively dead **and** 2027 OEP enrollment down >10% again → cut THC to zero, halve UHS.

## 8. Verdict & the price/fact that changes it

**UHS: PURSUE.** At ~$156 / 6.6x guided EPS / 5.4x EBITDA, the bear case (§5) roughly equals the current price and the base case offers ~55% upside, with a 9%-of-cap annual buyback compounding the gap while you wait, and a free option on any subsidy compromise or FL/CA DPP approvals. This is *not* the clean "secular demand priced as cyclical" story in the backlog — roughly half of EBITDA is policy-dependent and the behavioral franchise carries genuine tort risk — so it should be sized as a statistical cheap with a tail (half-position, e.g., 2–3% not 6–8%), not as a compounder. It is the correct leg of the pair *today*.
- *The price that changes it:* above ~$200 (≈8.5x) the margin of safety vs the supplemental/tort tails is gone without new facts.
- *The fact that changes it:* any §7 kill criterion — accelerated supplemental cuts (>$150M/yr ahead of schedule), a second nine-figure verdict wave, or two quarters of declining acute same-facility EBITDA. Also revisit if the July 27 Q2 print cuts FY26 guidance on *volume* (not weather) grounds.

**THC: WATCH.** The better business — ~47% of EBITDA from the #1 ASC platform with a real secular runway, 2.24x levered, ~7.5x shareholder FCF, 8%/yr share shrink — but the market has already paid for the recovery (+19% y/y, 11.6x forward, ~15–20% to base value). The backlog's 9.7x entry no longer exists. Buy order at **≤$180** (~10x forward, ~30% to base), or on any 2027-exchange-cliff panic that hits the hospital segment headline while leaving USPI intact — that would be a gift.

**Pair conclusion:** UHS is the better *risk/reward*; THC is the better *business*. If forced to hold one for ten years at these prices, UHS's 6.6x with a hard buyback beats THC's 11.6x by enough to compensate for the hair — but only at half weight, with the §7 tripwires live.
